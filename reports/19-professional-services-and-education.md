# Professional services and education — 20/20 data refinery sector report

## Bottom line

**Why financially attractive in the 2020s (measured evidence):** Professional services combine high-skill labor, recurring enterprise contracts, and relatively low physical-capital intensity. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects employment in professional, scientific, and technical services (NAICS 54) to grow **10.5% from 2023–33**, versus **4.2% for total wage-and-salary employment**; computer systems design is projected to grow **19.5%**, with BLS citing IT outsourcing, cybersecurity, and advanced-technology/AI adoption as demand drivers ([BLS, fastest-growing industry sector](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2025/article/fastest-growing-industry-sector.htm)). The sector already employed about **10.84 million** people in September 2026, with average hourly earnings of **$56.45** in August 2026 ([BLS industry profile](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm)).

Education is a large, comparatively non-discretionary spending pool with durable demand for credentials, assessment, workforce reskilling, and school services. U.S. public elementary and secondary schools spent **$927 billion in 2020–21** (constant 2022–23 dollars), including **$16,280 current expenditure per pupil**; purchased services represented **11%** of current expenditure and included transportation, food, janitorial services, and teacher professional development ([NCES expenditures](https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=66)). Postsecondary demand is mixed rather than uniformly growing: the 18–24 college enrollment rate was **39% in 2022**, down from 41% in 2012, while four-year enrollment rose from 28% to 31% and two-year enrollment fell from 13% to 9% ([NCES college enrollment](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cpb)).

**Profitability evidence:** Leading companies show that scalable intellectual property, managed services, software/platform economics, and recurring employer contracts can produce attractive margins. Accenture reported FY2024 revenue of **$64.9 billion**, GAAP operating margin of **14.8%**, adjusted operating margin of **15.5%**, free cash flow of **$8.6 billion**, and bookings of **$81.2 billion**; it specifically describes client demand for technology, data, AI, cloud, cybersecurity, and managed services ([Accenture FY2024 annual report](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/a-com-custom-component/iconic/document/Accenture-Fiscal-2024-Annual-Report.pdf)). ADP reported FY2024 revenue of **$19.20 billion**; Employer Services revenue grew 8% to **$12.98 billion** and its earnings-before-tax margin was **35.1%**, supported by new business bookings, client retention, pricing, operating efficiencies, and interest on client funds ([ADP FY2024 Form 10-K](https://s205.q4cdn.com/887941133/files/doc_financials/2024/ar/adp-fy24-10-k.pdf)). Pearson reported 2024 sales of **£3.552 billion**, adjusted operating profit of **£600 million**, a **16.9% adjusted operating-profit margin**, and £490 million free cash flow; it also expanded AI-enabled higher-education courseware, English-language learning, virtual learning, assessment, and workforce-skills offerings ([Pearson 2024 annual report](https://plc.pearson.com/sites/pearson-corp/files/pearson/annual-report-2024/2024-20-F.pdf)).

These figures support a **profit-pool thesis**, not a blanket claim that every education or professional-services business is profitable. Education providers exposed to enrollment declines, public budgets, labor costs, student-aid rules, or weak outcomes can be structurally less attractive.

## Most relevant industries

### Professional services

1. **Computer systems design, IT consulting, cloud migration, cybersecurity, data engineering, and AI implementation.** This is the clearest measured growth pocket: BLS projects 19.5% employment growth for computer systems design from 2023–33 and explicitly links demand to outsourcing, cybersecurity, and AI ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2025/article/fastest-growing-industry-sector.htm)).
2. **Management, scientific, and technical consulting.** Demand is tied to regulation, operating-model change, digital transformation, and specialized expertise. BLS lists this as a core NAICS 5416 industry ([BLS NAICS 54 profile](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm)).
3. **Accounting, tax preparation, bookkeeping, and payroll/HCM.** Payroll and compliance are recurring, mission-critical workflows. ADP’s FY2024 results provide measured evidence of scale and margin in employer services ([ADP FY2024 Form 10-K](https://s205.q4cdn.com/887941133/files/doc_financials/2024/ar/adp-fy24-10-k.pdf)).
4. **Engineering, architecture, and technical design.** Infrastructure, energy transition, industrial automation, and compliance create project and recurring advisory demand; BLS includes architectural/engineering and specialized design as distinct NAICS 5413/5414 groups ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm)).
5. **Legal services and audit/advisory.** Regulation and litigation are relatively resilient demand sources, though partner compensation and labor intensity can limit operating leverage. BLS includes legal services and accounting among the sector’s nine industry groups ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm)).
6. **Scientific R&D, testing, certification, and regulatory services.** These benefit from complex technology, life sciences, product safety, and compliance requirements; BLS identifies scientific R&D as NAICS 5417 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm)).
7. **Advertising, marketing, and customer-experience services.** Digital measurement and personalization support demand, but these are more cyclical and price-competitive than compliance or payroll services; BLS identifies advertising as NAICS 5418 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm)).

### Education

1. **K–12 contracted services and education technology.** The NCES spending base is large, and purchased services are a measurable procurement pool ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=66)).
2. **Assessment, testing, credentials, and workforce skills verification.** These can monetize high-stakes decisions and employer demand for measurable skills. Pearson’s annual report documents assessment, skills measurement, credentialing, and enterprise learning as core activities ([Pearson](https://plc.pearson.com/sites/pearson-corp/files/pearson/annual-report-2024/2024-20-F.pdf)).
3. **Digital courseware, virtual learning, and English-language learning.** Scalable content and software can improve economics versus purely instructional labor, but retention and learning outcomes are decisive.
4. **Corporate learning, professional certification, and reskilling.** AI and technology shifts create recurring employer demand for upskilling; Pearson reports deals with ServiceNow, Degreed, Microsoft, and AWS to prepare workers for the future of work ([Pearson](https://plc.pearson.com/sites/pearson-corp/files/pearson/annual-report-2024/2024-20-F.pdf)).
5. **Higher education services and student-support infrastructure.** This is a large addressable market, but enrollment and affordability vary by institution and demographic; NCES reports the 2022 enrollment-rate decline and divergent two-year/four-year trends ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cpb)).
6. **Tutoring, supplemental instruction, and learning recovery.** Demand is plausible where achievement gaps persist, but public evidence should be assessed locally and by outcome rather than assumed from enrollment alone.

## Candidate leading companies (not a ranking)

The following are **research candidates**, selected because they have visible scale, relevant products, public filings, or measurable customer signals. This is not a league table and does not assert that any company is the sector leader.

- **Accenture** — technology consulting, managed services, strategy, cloud, cybersecurity, data, and AI; FY2024 financial scale and margin are disclosed in its annual report ([source](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/a-com-custom-component/iconic/document/Accenture-Fiscal-2024-Annual-Report.pdf)).
- **Automatic Data Processing (ADP)** — payroll, HCM, employer services, and PEO; FY2024 Employer Services margin was 35.1% ([source](https://s205.q4cdn.com/887941133/files/doc_financials/2024/ar/adp-fy24-10-k.pdf)).
- **Deloitte, PwC, EY, and KPMG** — audit, tax, risk, consulting, and transactions; private partnership structures mean public financial comparability is limited. Use official transparency reports and country-level filings where available.
- **IBM Consulting, Capgemini, CGI, TCS, Infosys, and Cognizant** — enterprise IT services and transformation; compare reported growth, bookings, utilization, attrition, and margin in their free annual reports/filings.
- **Pearson** — assessment, learning content, higher education, English-language learning, virtual learning, and workforce skills; FY2024 results are measurable in its 20-F ([source](https://plc.pearson.com/sites/pearson-corp/files/pearson/annual-report-2024/2024-20-F.pdf)).
- **Duolingo** — consumer language learning subscription and app-based education; assess paid-subscriber growth, bookings, retention, and profitability from SEC filings.
- **Coursera and Udemy** — online learning marketplaces and enterprise learning; assess registered learners versus paid conversion, enterprise net retention, gross margin, and cash burn from SEC filings.
- **Chegg** — digital student support and study tools; a useful cautionary candidate because generative AI can both expand and disrupt education platforms; inspect its filings rather than assuming growth.
- **Instructure** — learning-management software (Canvas) for institutions and enterprises; recurring software revenue and retention make it a relevant education-infrastructure candidate.
- **Strategic Education, Grand Canyon Education, and other public postsecondary providers** — candidate operators/services businesses, but outcomes, regulation, enrollment mix, and student-aid exposure must be evaluated carefully.

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals

### Professional services and B2B platforms

- **Merchant-services satisfaction is measurable at the customer level.** J.D. Power’s 2024 U.S. Merchant Services Satisfaction Study surveyed **5,383 small-business customers** across six dimensions. Shopify scored **728**, Paysafe 725, and Bank of America 713 in that study; the study found satisfaction of 692 for credit-card processing and 694 for debit-card processing, versus 744 where BNPL was processed ([J.D. Power merchant-services study](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2024-us-merchant-services-satisfaction-study/)). These are measured scores for that study and should not be generalized to all professional services.
- **Advisor satisfaction and retention intent are useful workforce/customer proxies for wealth-management professional services.** J.D. Power surveyed **4,072 employee and independent financial advisors** in 2024. Employee-advisor satisfaction rose 49 points to 637; independent-advisor satisfaction fell 15 points to 611. Stifel ranked highest among employee-advisor firms at 767, while Commonwealth ranked highest among independent firms at 819. The study also found 34% of employee advisors and 41% of independent advisors more than two years from retirement may not stay at their firm in the next one to two years ([J.D. Power financial-advisor study](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2024-us-financial-advisor-satisfaction-study/)). This is primarily an advisor-experience signal, not an end-client satisfaction score.
- **Operational customer signals to collect from filings:** client retention, net revenue retention, bookings/book-to-bill, renewal rates, backlog, implementation success, service-level attainment, NPS/CSAT where disclosed, complaints, churn, utilization, employee attrition, and pricing realization. Accenture itself warns that profitability depends on maintaining demand, adapting services, pricing, delivery efficiency, and satisfying agreed service levels ([Accenture FY2024 annual report](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/a-com-custom-component/iconic/document/Accenture-Fiscal-2024-Annual-Report.pdf)).

### Education

- **Completion, persistence, placement, licensure/pass rates, learning gains, and student-loan repayment** are stronger quality signals than enrollment or marketing reach alone. Compare these with price, demographics, and program mix.
- **Student satisfaction is informative but not sufficient.** For institutions and digital providers, collect independently administered student surveys, course completion, active use, renewal, refunds, complaint rates, transfer rates, and verified employment/earnings outcomes. NCES enrollment statistics show why demand should not be inferred from enrollment alone: overall 18–24 enrollment was 39% in 2022, with four-year and two-year trends moving in opposite directions ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cpb)).
- **Public procurement and budget signals matter in K–12.** NCES reports $927 billion total public-school expenditure and 11% of current expenditure on purchased services in 2020–21 ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=66)). For vendors, district renewal rates, contract expansion, implementation milestones, independent efficacy evidence, and state/district concentration are more decision-useful than total addressable market claims.
- **Company-reported product and employee measures can corroborate, not replace, customer evidence.** Pearson reports AI-enabled product development and a 2024 internal manager Coaching Index of 4.08/5, up from 3.95; the latter is an employee measure, not student satisfaction ([Pearson](https://plc.pearson.com/sites/pearson-corp/files/pearson/annual-report-2024/2024-20-F.pdf)).

## Data fields available for the 20/20 refinery

- **Scale:** revenue, bookings, backlog, employment, establishments, enrollment, per-pupil spending, geographic mix.
- **Profitability:** gross/operating/EBITDA margin, cash flow, cash conversion, recurring-revenue share, utilization, price realization, customer acquisition cost, and capital intensity.
- **Growth:** BLS industry employment projections, company organic growth, paid users, enrollments, course starts/completions, enterprise net retention, new-business bookings, and contract renewals.
- **Quality:** NPS/CSAT, J.D. Power or other independent scores, complaints, refunds, retention/churn, course completion, persistence, licensure/pass rates, verified outcomes, student debt/repayment, and service-level attainment.
- **Risk:** labor scarcity and wage inflation, AI substitution/disintermediation, public-budget dependence, enrollment/demographic decline, regulatory changes, privacy/cybersecurity, pricing pressure, and outcomes-based scrutiny.

## Interpretation and caveats

- **The sector label combines two economically different markets.** Professional services are mostly fee-for-expertise and enterprise/B2B; education mixes public spending, tuition, subscriptions, grants, and employer budgets. Do not treat them as one homogeneous margin pool.
- **Measured company margins are not sector averages.** Accenture, ADP, and Pearson are selected public-company examples with different accounting, mix, geographic exposure, and business models.
- **BLS projections are employment projections, not revenue or profit forecasts.** Higher employment growth can coexist with wage pressure, automation, or lower pricing.
- **Education spending is not vendor revenue.** The NCES $927 billion figure includes public-school salaries, benefits, capital, supplies, and services; only a subset is addressable by commercial providers.
- **Customer-quality rankings are study-specific.** J.D. Power rankings apply to surveyed brands, samples, dates, and dimensions. They are not universal rankings of professional-services quality.
- **Free public data have coverage limits.** Partnership-owned firms may lack comparable audited financials; public-company disclosure can emphasize adjusted metrics; student outcomes may be delayed, incomplete, or confounded by student selection and institutional mix.
