# Sector 13 — E-commerce and marketplaces

## Bottom line

**Assessment: highly attractive, but not uniformly profitable.** E-commerce and marketplaces are financially lucrative in the 2020s because digital demand is still taking share from physical retail, while scaled platforms can monetize the same transaction through several high-margin layers: seller commissions and services, advertising, payments, subscriptions, logistics, and adjacent financial products. The strongest economics accrue to businesses with network effects, repeat purchase behavior, differentiated assortment or community, and enough volume to spread fulfillment and technology costs. This is a sector judgment, not a measured industry ranking.

## Why the sector is financially lucrative/profitable

1. **Large and growing transaction base.** The U.S. Census Bureau reported seasonally adjusted retail e-commerce sales of **$340.2 billion in Q2 2026**, up **12.2% year over year**, and equal to **17.1% of total retail sales** ([Census Bureau, Quarterly Retail E-Commerce Sales](https://www.census.gov/retail/ecommerce.html)). This is a measured U.S. indicator rather than a global market-size estimate, but it demonstrates continuing channel penetration and growth in the 2020s.

2. **Marketplace operating leverage.** A marketplace can earn commissions and service revenue without owning all inventory. eBay reported **$74.7 billion of 2024 GMV**, **$10.3 billion revenue**, **22.5% GAAP operating margin**, and **$2.0 billion free cash flow** ([eBay FY2024 results](https://investors.ebayinc.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2025/eBay-Inc.-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2024-Results/default.aspx)). The result is direct evidence that a mature, asset-light marketplace can be highly cash generative, although eBay's margin should not be generalized to every platform.

3. **Multiple monetization layers improve unit economics.** Amazon's 2024 filing groups third-party seller commissions and related fulfillment/shipping fees in service sales and separately reports advertising and subscription services. In 2024, Amazon generated **$638.0 billion net sales**, **$68.6 billion operating income**, and **$115.9 billion operating cash flow** ([Amazon 2024 results](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2025/Amazon-com-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-Results/)). Amazon is a diversified company, so these totals are not pure marketplace profitability; the evidence supports the monetization model, not a claim that all operating income comes from e-commerce.

4. **Network effects and repeat usage.** More sellers create broader assortment; broader assortment attracts buyers; buyer traffic attracts sellers and advertisers. This is an economic mechanism and therefore a judgment about causality, but it is consistent with observed scale metrics. eBay ended 2024 with **134 million active buyers** and **2.3 billion live listings** in its 10-K ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065088/000106508825000037/ebay-20241231.htm)). Mercado Libre crossed **100 million annual unique buyers** in 2024 and reported **$51.5 billion GMV**, up **15%** ([Mercado Libre FY2024 results](https://news.mercadolibre.com/en/financial-results-fourth-quarter-2024)).

5. **Payments, logistics, and advertising expand revenue per customer.** Mercado Libre's ecosystem illustrates the flywheel: in Q4 2024, advertising revenue grew **41% year over year** and reached **2.1% of GMV**; Mercado Pago monthly active users exceeded **61 million**, while its 2024 total payment volume reached **$197 billion** ([Mercado Libre FY2024 results](https://news.mercadolibre.com/en/financial-results-fourth-quarter-2024)). The company reported **$2.6 billion full-year operating income** and a **12.7% operating margin**, but this includes fintech and should not be treated as marketplace-only margin.

6. **Digital distribution and data can support high-margin adjacencies.** Search, sponsored listings, seller tools, subscriptions, payments, and consumer credit can be sold against an existing user and transaction base. This is a sector-level judgment; actual margin and risk vary substantially by category, geography, fraud losses, credit underwriting, returns, and delivery intensity.

## Most relevant industries / subsectors

- **General merchandise marketplaces:** broad horizontal platforms connecting many sellers and buyers; examples include Amazon Marketplace, eBay, Walmart Marketplace, and Mercado Libre.
- **Specialty and vertical marketplaces:** fashion, luxury, beauty, home, pets, electronics, collectibles, used goods, tickets, and professional services. Vertical focus can improve discovery, trust, and take rates; it can also narrow the addressable market.
- **Classifieds and recommerce:** used cars, real estate, jobs, local services, and second-hand goods. Revenue is often listing, lead, subscription, or transaction based rather than retail gross merchandise sales.
- **Direct-to-consumer and omnichannel commerce infrastructure:** merchant storefronts, checkout, payments, fulfillment, and marketing software. Shopify is a candidate infrastructure/platform company rather than a conventional inventory retailer ([Shopify 2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1594805/000159480525000012/shop-20241231.htm)).
- **Digital advertising within commerce:** sponsored products, retail media, brand ads, and first-party measurement. It is especially relevant because advertising can carry higher incremental margins than product fulfillment.
- **Payments, logistics, and fulfillment attached to commerce:** payment processing, last-mile delivery, fulfillment-as-a-service, and embedded credit. These can deepen platform retention but introduce financial, regulatory, fraud, and capital risks.
- **Cross-border e-commerce:** international sellers and buyers, localization, duties, payments, and delivery. Growth can be strong, but FX, regulation, customs, and geopolitical exposure are material.

## Candidate leading companies

These are **candidates for diligence**, not an invented ranking. They are selected for scale, disclosed marketplace/commerce metrics, geographic relevance, or differentiated business model.

- **Amazon** — global general merchandise, third-party marketplace, logistics, subscriptions, advertising, and cloud; 2024 net sales $638.0B and operating income $68.6B ([official results](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2025/Amazon-com-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-Results/)).
- **eBay** — mature, asset-light global marketplace; 2024 GMV $74.7B, 134M active buyers, and 22.5% GAAP operating margin ([official results](https://investors.ebayinc.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2025/eBay-Inc.-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2024-Results/default.aspx); [SEC 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065088/000106508825000037/ebay-20241231.htm)).
- **Mercado Libre** — Latin American marketplace plus payments, logistics, ads, and credit; 2024 GMV $51.5B and 100M+ annual unique buyers ([official results](https://news.mercadolibre.com/en/financial-results-fourth-quarter-2024)).
- **Alibaba** — China-centered marketplaces, international commerce, logistics and cloud; use its free annual reports and SEC filings for current metrics ([SEC company filings](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1577552&owner=exclude)).
- **JD.com** — China e-commerce and integrated logistics; useful comparison for inventory-led versus marketplace-led economics ([SEC company filings](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1549802&owner=exclude)).
- **Shopify** — merchant commerce infrastructure, payments, and ecosystem; a platform/infrastructure candidate rather than a marketplace-only peer ([2024 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1594805/000159480525000012/shop-20241231.htm)).
- **Etsy** — differentiated handmade, vintage, and creative-goods marketplace; specialty marketplace candidate ([SEC company filings](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1370637&owner=exclude)).
- **Walmart** — omnichannel retailer with a growing marketplace and fulfillment/advertising ecosystem; candidate for scale and store-enabled fulfillment comparison ([SEC company filings](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=104169&owner=exclude)).
- **PDD Holdings / Temu** — value-oriented, cross-border marketplace candidate; diligence should emphasize regulatory, merchant-quality, logistics, and customer-trust risks ([SEC company filings](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1737806&owner=exclude)).

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals

Customer quality is best treated as a **dashboard**, not a single ranking. Relevant signals include repeat purchase frequency, active buyers, buyer growth, conversion, order frequency, retention, net promoter score, complaint/return rates, on-time delivery, cancellation and refund rates, seller quality, fraud/chargebacks, app/site reliability, checkout ease, product-review helpfulness, and customer-support resolution.

A reputable public benchmark is the American Customer Satisfaction Index (ACSI). Its latest online-retailer benchmark lists an overall industry score of **79**, based on data collected January–December 2025. Company scores include **Amazon 82, eBay 78, Chewy 82, Etsy 77, Walmart 77, and Temu 74**; the benchmark covers 22 major online retailers plus smaller sites ([ACSI Online Retailers](https://theacsi.com/industries/retail/online-retailers/)). These are measured satisfaction scores, not a profitability ranking, and the scores are not directly comparable across every business model or country.

ACSI also reports operational experience measures for online retail: **mobile-app quality 88, app reliability 87, checkout/payment ease 86, navigation ease 84, delivery quality 83, site performance 83, product-image usefulness 83, product-description usefulness 82, merchandise variety 82, and return ease 80**. These are useful leading indicators because they connect customer experience to conversion and repeat usage, but they do not establish causation by themselves.

Company disclosures can provide complementary behavioral and quality signals. eBay discloses active buyers and GMV; Mercado Libre discloses buyer growth, same/next-day delivery penetration, items sold, and NPS; Amazon discloses customer and seller definitions and operating metrics in its filings. For example, Mercado Libre reported **49% of deliveries completed same or next day** in 2024 and record NPS levels in Brazil and Mexico in Q4 2024 ([official results](https://news.mercadolibre.com/en/financial-results-fourth-quarter-2024)). Treat these as company-reported indicators, not independently audited satisfaction rankings.

## Metrics available for the 20/20 data refinery

- **Demand/scale:** GMV, revenue, orders, units sold, active buyers, annual unique buyers, monthly active users, listings, and buyer frequency.
- **Monetization:** take rate (revenue divided by GMV, with definition checked), seller-services revenue, advertising revenue, subscription revenue, payment volume, payment take rate, and logistics revenue.
- **Profitability/cash:** gross margin, contribution margin, operating margin, EBITDA where disclosed, operating cash flow, free cash flow, fulfillment cost per order, and sales/marketing per new buyer.
- **Customer quality:** ACSI or other reputable satisfaction score, NPS, repeat rate, cohort retention, app/site reliability, checkout ease, delivery speed and condition, returns/refunds, complaints, fraud/chargebacks, and support resolution.
- **Marketplace health:** active sellers, seller retention, assortment breadth, concentration, buyer-to-seller liquidity, conversion, ad penetration, and percentage of GMV fulfilled or payments processed by the platform.
- **Risk/quality controls:** counterfeit and trust-and-safety incidents, regulatory actions, privacy/security incidents, credit losses, bad debt, cross-border/FX exposure, and dependence on paid traffic or subsidies.

## Caveats and interpretation

- **Do not rank companies from these data alone.** Amazon's consolidated profit includes AWS and other businesses; Mercado Libre includes fintech; Walmart and JD.com have substantial inventory-led retail. Comparisons require segment-level normalization.
- **GMV is not revenue.** GMV measures transaction value; platforms recognize only commissions, services, or net merchandise revenue depending on the accounting model. Always inspect each company's definition.
- **Growth can be bought.** Free shipping, coupons, subsidies, paid acquisition, and low take rates can raise GMV while destroying contribution margin. Check retention and contribution economics.
- **Customer satisfaction is not customer quality.** A high ACSI score can coexist with weak growth or low margins; repeat rate, complaint rates, returns, and contribution profit should be triangulated.
- **Public-company disclosure is selective.** Active buyers, NPS, take rates, advertising revenue, and marketplace-only margins are not disclosed consistently. Missing data should be marked unavailable rather than estimated without a stated method.
- **The Census figure is U.S.-only and the cited page currently shows the latest release, not a complete global series.** Use country-specific official statistics when comparing geographies.
