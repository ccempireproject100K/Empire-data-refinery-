# Artificial intelligence and data infrastructure

## Bottom line

**Sector thesis:** Artificial intelligence and data infrastructure is financially attractive in the 2020s because AI adoption is increasing demand for compute, networking, storage, cloud platforms, data warehouses, cybersecurity, and power-dense data centers, while software/platform layers can monetize recurring consumption and high switching costs. The strongest measured evidence is concentrated in infrastructure suppliers and cloud/data platforms; profitability is not uniform across the sector, especially for capital-intensive facilities and early-stage model developers.

This is a **sector assessment, not a definitive ranking**. “Leading” below means a plausible candidate based on disclosed scale, growth, strategic position, and customer traction—not an invented league table.

## Why it can be lucrative/profitable in the 2020s

1. **Demand is broadening from experimentation to production.** Stanford HAI’s [2025 AI Index](https://hai-production.s3.amazonaws.com/files/hai_ai_index_report_2025.pdf) reports that 78% of organizations said they used AI in 2024, versus 55% in 2023. Flexera’s [2024 State of the Cloud](https://www.flexera.com/about-us/press-center/flexera-2024-state-of-the-cloud-managing-spending-top-challenge) survey of 753 cloud decision-makers found 89% using multi-cloud, 49% using AWS for significant workloads, 45% Azure, and 21% Google Cloud; 41% reported ML/AI platform-as-a-service usage, with another 32% experimenting and 17% planning to use it. These are survey measures, not total-market shares.

2. **AI workload intensity is creating a large infrastructure pull-through.** The [IEA Energy and AI analysis](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai/energy-demand-from-ai) estimates data centers consumed about 415 TWh, or 1.5% of global electricity, in 2024 and projects roughly 945 TWh by 2030 in its base case. Accelerated-server electricity use, mainly driven by AI, is projected to grow 30% annually and account for almost half of the net increase in data-center consumption. This supports demand for GPUs, CPUs, networking, cooling, colocation, power procurement, and efficiency software, although it also creates permitting, power, and capex risks.

3. **Performance-per-dollar is improving, expanding the addressable market.** Stanford’s AI Index reports that the inference cost for a system performing at GPT-3.5 level fell from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens between November 2022 and October 2024, a more-than-280-fold decline. Lower unit costs can increase usage and make enterprise deployment economic, even while frontier training and data-center investment remain expensive.

4. **Some platform businesses show operating leverage and recurring expansion.** NVIDIA reported FY2025 revenue of $130.5 billion, up 114%, 75.0% GAAP gross margin, $81.5 billion operating income, and $115.2 billion full-year Data Center revenue, up 142% ([official results](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2025)). Microsoft reported FY2025 revenue of $281.7 billion, operating income of $128.5 billion, Azure revenue above $75 billion, and Azure growth of 34% ([2025 annual report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html)). These are company-specific results, not proof that every AI infrastructure company is profitable.

5. **Consumption and ecosystem lock-in can raise customer lifetime value.** Snowflake reported FY2025 Q4 product revenue growth of 28%, 126% net revenue retention, 580 customers above $1 million of trailing-12-month product revenue, 745 Forbes Global 2000 customers, and $6.9 billion of remaining performance obligations ([SEC-filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1640147/000164014725000016/fy2025q4earnings.htm)). Its filing explains that product revenue is tied to customer consumption of compute, storage, and data transfer, making usage expansion a measurable value/retention signal. Note that Snowflake still reported a GAAP operating loss in that quarter; strong retention does not equal GAAP profitability.

## Most relevant industries and demand pools

- **Cloud computing and hyperscale data centers:** infrastructure-as-a-service, platform services, managed AI, storage, databases, and networking. The main demand pool is enterprise migration plus new AI workloads.
- **Semiconductors and accelerated computing:** GPUs, custom accelerators, CPUs, DPUs, memory, interconnects, and complete AI systems. NVIDIA’s [FY2025 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000023/nvda-20250126.htm) describes a full-stack platform spanning chips, networking, software, and managed DGX Cloud; it also identifies public cloud, consumer internet, enterprises, startups, and the public sector as customers.
- **Data platforms and analytics:** cloud data warehouses, lakehouses, data integration, governance, observability, vector search, and model-serving infrastructure. These benefit as enterprises need trusted data to train, ground, monitor, and audit AI systems.
- **Colocation, power, cooling, and data-center construction:** AI’s higher rack density and electricity demand create demand for specialized facilities, liquid cooling, grid interconnection, backup power, and energy-efficiency solutions. The IEA’s [analysis](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai/energy-demand-from-ai) explicitly notes long lead times and high upfront investment.
- **Enterprise software and cybersecurity:** AI copilots, developer tools, identity, security analytics, data-loss prevention, and AI governance. These can monetize per-seat subscriptions or usage while increasing the value of underlying cloud/data infrastructure.
- **Financial services, healthcare/life sciences, manufacturing, retail, telecom, automotive, and government:** high-value use cases include fraud/risk, medical imaging and drug discovery, industrial automation, demand forecasting, customer service, network optimization, and public-service delivery. NVIDIA’s filing lists partnerships across automotive, healthcare, financial services, manufacturing, retail, and technology; Microsoft’s annual report provides examples including healthcare, banking, justice, retail, and automotive customer deployments.

## Candidate leading companies

These are **candidates for further diligence**, not a measured ranking:

- **NVIDIA** — accelerated compute, networking, AI systems, CUDA/software, and managed AI infrastructure. FY2025 disclosed Data Center revenue of $115.2 billion and 75.0% GAAP gross margin ([official results](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2025)).
- **Microsoft** — Azure, AI services, enterprise distribution, and data/AI applications. FY2025 Azure revenue exceeded $75 billion and grew 34% ([annual report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html)).
- **Amazon Web Services (Amazon)** — broad cloud infrastructure, compute, storage, databases, and managed AI services. Flexera’s survey found AWS was used for significant workloads by 49% of respondents ([survey release](https://www.flexera.com/about-us/press-center/flexera-2024-state-of-the-cloud-managing-spending-top-challenge)); this is adoption evidence, not market-share measurement.
- **Alphabet/Google Cloud** — hyperscale infrastructure, AI accelerators, data/analytics, and model services. Candidate status rests on platform breadth and AI ecosystem; verify current segment revenue and margin directly in Alphabet’s latest [SEC filings](https://abc.xyz/investor/).
- **Meta Platforms** — one of the largest AI infrastructure buyers and operators, with potential strategic importance in compute, recommendation systems, and generative AI. It is more an infrastructure-scale operator and technology platform than a pure-play infrastructure vendor.
- **Broadcom** — custom AI accelerators, networking, and connectivity; candidate exposure to hyperscaler-specific infrastructure demand. Validate current AI revenue disclosure in Broadcom’s [SEC filings](https://investors.broadcom.com/financial-information/sec-filings).
- **Arista Networks** — high-speed data-center networking; candidate beneficiary of AI cluster interconnect demand. Validate current mix and customer concentration in its [annual reports](https://investors.arista.com/financials/annual-reports-and-proxies/default.aspx).
- **Snowflake** — cloud data platform with consumption expansion and AI/data workloads. FY2025 Q4 disclosed 126% net revenue retention and $6.9 billion RPO, alongside a GAAP operating loss ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1640147/000164014725000016/fy2025q4earnings.htm)).
- **Datadog, MongoDB, and Cloudflare** — candidate software/data infrastructure beneficiaries through observability, databases, edge delivery, security, and developer workloads. Treat them as adjacent platform candidates and compare disclosed retention, margins, and free cash flow rather than assuming uniform AI exposure.
- **Equinix, Digital Realty, and Vertiv** — candidate physical-infrastructure beneficiaries through colocation, interconnection, power, cooling, and data-center equipment. Their economics are more capital-intensive and sensitive to power availability, construction costs, and customer concentration.

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals to track

There is no single free, authoritative customer-satisfaction league table for enterprise AI infrastructure. Use a **signal set**, and distinguish audited company metrics from marketing claims:

- **Net revenue retention (NRR):** best public expansion/retention signal for subscription or consumption platforms. Snowflake’s 126% NRR is measured and company-reported; it indicates that the defined existing-customer cohort generated 26% more product revenue year over year, but it is not an independent satisfaction survey.
- **Large-customer penetration and concentration:** counts of customers above $1 million, Global 2000 customers, Fortune 500 customers, or cloud commitments indicate enterprise acceptance. Snowflake’s 580 $1m+ and 745 Forbes Global 2000 customers are useful disclosed signals, with definitional caveats in the filing.
- **Remaining performance obligations, backlog, and contracted commitments:** useful for visibility but not proof of successful implementation or renewal. Snowflake’s $6.9 billion RPO is a measured forward-commitment signal.
- **Usage growth and workload migration:** consumption growth, active customers, seats, API calls, data processed, and production workloads show actual use better than press-release logos. For Snowflake, product revenue is consumption-linked; for cloud providers, disclosed Azure/AWS/Google Cloud growth and workloads are relevant but segment disclosures differ.
- **Independent review evidence:** public G2 or similar review scores can provide directional user sentiment, but review populations are self-selected and category definitions vary. Do not compare scores across vendors without checking the methodology, number of reviews, date, customer role, and product scope. G2’s research/methodology pages are available at [G2 Research](https://research.g2.com/).
- **Operational quality:** uptime/SLA attainment, incident frequency, latency, support response, security certifications, and data-loss/security incidents are closer to service quality than generic satisfaction. Prefer provider status histories, contractual SLA terms, and regulator/auditor disclosures over vendor case-study quotations.
- **Customer outcomes:** quantified customer time saved, cost reduced, revenue added, or error rates improved are persuasive when independently verifiable. Microsoft’s FY2025 report, for example, says Carvana reduced inbound calls per sale by 45% and Mercy saved caregivers over 100,000 hours; these are vendor-reported case examples, not independent studies ([annual report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html)).

## Key risks and caveats

- **Cyclicality and concentration:** NVIDIA disclosed that three direct customers represented 12%, 11%, and 11% of FY2025 total revenue, respectively ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000023/nvda-20250126.htm)). Hyperscaler capex pauses or customer concentration can reverse growth quickly.
- **Capital intensity and bottlenecks:** power, chips, networking, memory, cooling, land, permits, and grid connections can constrain deployments. IEA explicitly notes uncertainty, long energy-system lead times, and high upfront investment.
- **Margin compression:** Microsoft said gross-margin percentage declined in FY2025 in Intelligent Cloud because of scaling AI infrastructure ([annual report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html)). High demand can coexist with lower near-term margins when capacity is being built.
- **Rapid technology transitions:** NVIDIA’s filing warns that product transitions, inventory, qualification cycles, and supply commitments can create revenue volatility and inventory provisions.
- **Model and software commoditization:** falling inference costs and improving open-weight models can expand total usage but pressure pricing and differentiate less at the model/API layer.
- **Regulation, security, privacy, and reliability:** AI incidents, copyright/privacy obligations, export controls, cybersecurity failures, and sector-specific regulation can delay deployments or increase compliance cost.
- **Evidence quality:** company-reported metrics are useful but promotional; survey adoption is not revenue; review ratings are selection-biased; and “leading” candidates should be re-tested against the latest filings before investment use.
