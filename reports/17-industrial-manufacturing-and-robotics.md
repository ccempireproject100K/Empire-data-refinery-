# Industrial manufacturing and robotics

## Bottom line

**Why this sector can be financially lucrative in the 2020s:** robotics and industrial automation sell productivity, capacity, quality, safety, and labor substitution/complementation to customers that face persistent labor constraints and increasingly complex production requirements. The opportunity is not a claim that every robotics company is profitable: equipment orders are cyclical and 2023–24 exposed weakness in China and normalized post-shortage ordering. The attractive economics are strongest where suppliers combine differentiated hardware, software, integration, and recurring lifecycle services.

Measured evidence supports a large and expanding installed base. The International Federation of Robotics (IFR) reports **542,000 industrial robots installed globally in 2024**, more than double the 2014 level, and **4.664 million robots in operational use**, up 9% year over year. Asia represented 74% of 2024 installations; China alone represented 54%.[1](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years) IFR expects installations to reach 575,000 in 2025 and exceed 700,000 by 2028, but this is an industry forecast, not realized revenue.[1](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years)

The U.S. Census Bureau has created an unusually useful free dataset for this sector. Its experimental Annual Survey of Manufactures robotics product reports robot presence, workers exposed to robots, robot purchases, and capital expenditure on robotic equipment by manufacturing subsector, plant size, state, and year for 2018–2021.[2](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2024/econ/2021-asm-robotic-equipment.html) This is a strong source for measuring adoption and customer addressability, although it is explicitly experimental and has disclosure-avoidance/statistical-quality limitations.[2](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2024/econ/2021-asm-robotic-equipment.html)

## Profitability mechanisms and 2020s demand drivers

1. **Labor scarcity and higher required throughput.** NIST says automation can increase productivity, throughput and capacity, consistency, quality and yield, safety, and the amount of human effort available for higher-value work; it specifically notes the labor-shortage environment.[3](https://www.nist.gov/mep/robotics-and-manufacturing-automation) NIST’s 2025 manufacturing outlook says persistent labor shortages are pushing manufacturers toward automation to boost efficiency, reduce costs, and maintain production quality, while robots are becoming accessible to smaller manufacturers.[4](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/manufacturing-innovation-blog/whats-coming-us-manufacturing-2025) This supports customer ROI, but it does not by itself prove a vendor’s margin.

2. **Installed-base and lifecycle monetization.** Automation suppliers can sell controls, motion, vision, robots, integration, spare parts, upgrades, cybersecurity, predictive maintenance, and managed support. Rockwell’s fiscal-2024 filing describes Lifecycle Services as including recurring managed-support contracts, connected services, field services, and asset management; Lifecycle Services sales grew 10% and segment operating margin rose to 16.1% in 2024.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm) That is direct evidence for a potentially more resilient service layer, though Rockwell’s other segments declined in 2024.

3. **Quality, flexibility, and smaller lots.** ABB states that robotics and machine automation can provide productivity, quality, flexibility, and shorter cycles, while supporting smaller lots and a wider variety of products. It lists welding, material handling, machine tending, painting, picking, packing, palletizing, and assembly as applications.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm) These capabilities matter as manufacturers localize supply chains, add product variants, and automate mixed or high-mix production.

4. **Electrification, batteries, electronics, logistics, and reshoring.** BLS projects U.S. employment in “other electrical equipment and component manufacturing” to grow 37.7% from 2023 to 2033 and motor-vehicle manufacturing to grow 5.8%; it also projects manufacturing overall to grow 0.8%.[7](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2024/article/industry-and-occupational-employment-projections-overview-and-highlights-2023-33.htm) These figures are employment projections, not sales forecasts, but they identify automation-intensive production areas. ABB identifies automotive, machine building, metalworking, electronics, food and beverage, and logistics as core customer industries.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)

5. **Pricing power and solution content can offset input costs.** Rockwell reported that fiscal-2024 Intelligent Devices margin was 18.4% and Software & Control margin was 24.2%; management said price realization exceeded input costs, partially offsetting lower sales volume.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm) ABB’s Robotics & Discrete Automation revenue rose 14% to $3.640 billion in 2023, and operational EBITA rose 58% to $536 million; ABB attributed higher revenue partly to successful price increases and service demand.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm) These are company-specific results, not sector-wide averages, and should be treated as evidence of possible economics rather than a universal ranking.

## Most relevant industries and customer segments

- **Automotive, EVs, and batteries:** welding, assembly, painting, material handling, battery-cell/module/pack production, inspection, and end-of-line automation. BLS projects motor-vehicle manufacturing employment up 5.8% and other electrical equipment/component manufacturing up 37.7% through 2033.[7](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2024/article/industry-and-occupational-employment-projections-overview-and-highlights-2023-33.htm)
- **Electronics and semiconductors:** clean, precise handling, assembly, inspection, and factory automation; ABB and Rockwell both identify electronics/semiconductor markets.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm) [6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- **Machine builders and discrete manufacturing:** OEM machinery, motion control, PLCs, industrial PCs, machine vision, cobots, and integrated cells.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- **Food, beverage, consumer goods, and packaging:** hygienic handling, packaging, palletizing, inspection, and high-SKU flexibility.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm) [6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- **Logistics and warehousing:** autonomous mobile robots, picking, sorting, palletizing, and warehouse automation. IFR identifies logistics-related demand and ABB lists warehouse/logistics center automation.[1](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years) [6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- **Process industries:** energy, chemicals, mining, water, and related process control. Rockwell lists energy, mining, and chemicals as process end markets.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm)
- **Life sciences and medical manufacturing:** precise assembly, inspection, laboratory automation, and validated production; Rockwell identifies life sciences and ABB identifies life-sciences service applications.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm) [6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- **Metalworking and general industry:** machining, tending, welding, coating, and material removal, often attractive for repetitive, hazardous, or labor-constrained work.[3](https://www.nist.gov/mep/robotics-and-manufacturing-automation) [6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)

## Candidate leading companies

These are **candidates for diligence, not an invented ranking**. The measured basis is public segment scale, named competitive position, installed base, or industry presence. ABB’s filing explicitly names Fanuc, Kuka, Yaskawa, Epson, Dürr, Stäubli, Universal Robots, Rockwell Automation, Siemens, Mitsubishi Electric, and Beckhoff as competitors in robotics/discrete automation.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm) Rockwell names Siemens, ABB, Schneider Electric, Emerson, Mitsubishi Electric, Honeywell, AVEVA, Dassault Systèmes, and Aspen Technology among its competitors.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm)

- **ABB** — broad robotics, machine automation, motion, software, and services; 2023 Robotics & Discrete Automation revenue was $3.640 billion and operational EBITA $536 million.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- **Rockwell Automation** — industrial controls, intelligent devices, software/control, robotics through acquisitions, and lifecycle services; FY2024 sales were $8.264 billion, with segment margins disclosed in its 10-K.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm)
- **FANUC** — candidate industrial-robotics and CNC specialist; validate current segment revenue and margin in its free annual reports before ranking.
- **Yaskawa Electric / Motoman** — candidate robotics, motion-control, and drives supplier; Yaskawa Motoman reports more than 600,000 Motoman robots installed globally.[8](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/yaskawa-motoman-exceeds-expectations-for-annual-customer-survey-reflecting-the-companys-strong-commitment-to-customer-satisfaction)
- **KUKA** — candidate industrial-robotics and automation supplier, particularly automotive and logistics.
- **Siemens** — candidate factory/process automation and industrial software platform.
- **Schneider Electric** — candidate industrial automation, energy management, and software supplier.
- **Mitsubishi Electric** — candidate factory automation, motion, PLC, and robotics supplier.
- **Keyence** — candidate machine-vision and sensing beneficiary of automated inspection.
- **Honeywell, Emerson, and SMC** — candidates in process automation, controls, sensing, pneumatics, and connected industrial systems.
- **System integrators, machine builders, and software/service providers** — often the customer-facing implementation layer; ABB notes that sales flow both directly and through system integrators and machine builders.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals

The clearest freely accessible direct satisfaction signal found is Yaskawa Motoman’s 2023 customer-satisfaction survey: an overall corporate rating of **4.7 out of 5.0**, with year-over-year improvements reported in applications, customer satisfaction, marketing, sales, and standard products.[8](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/yaskawa-motoman-exceeds-expectations-for-annual-customer-survey-reflecting-the-companys-strong-commitment-to-customer-satisfaction) The source is an A3/Association for Advancing Automation article authored by Yaskawa America, so it is useful but self-reported and should not be treated as an independently audited cross-company benchmark.

Other practical quality signals are more measurable than generic satisfaction scores: robot installed base, repeat orders and backlog, service revenue, warranty/recall provisions, uptime and mean time between failures, on-time delivery, safety incidents, project gross margin, customer retention, net revenue retention for software/services, and third-party integrator references. Yaskawa’s reported installed base and ABB’s service revenue/order/backlog disclosure can serve as scale and repeat-use proxies, but neither proves satisfaction.[6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm) [8](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/yaskawa-motoman-exceeds-expectations-for-annual-customer-survey-reflecting-the-companys-strong-commitment-to-customer-satisfaction)

## Metrics available for the 20/20 data refinery

- IFR annual robot installations, operational stock, geography, country, industry, and forecast installations.[1](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years)
- Census ASM robotics: percent of plants with robots, workers exposed, robot purchases, robotic-equipment capital expenditures, plant-size distribution, subsector, state, and year.[2](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2024/econ/2021-asm-robotic-equipment.html)
- BLS employment levels and 2023–33 projections by manufacturing subsector, including EV/battery-adjacent industries.[7](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2024/article/industry-and-occupational-employment-projections-overview-and-highlights-2023-33.htm)
- Company revenue, orders, backlog, segment operating earnings/margins, price realization, service revenue, and geography from SEC filings.[5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1024478/000102447824000107/rok-20240930.htm) [6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- Customer-quality proxies: satisfaction scores, installed robots, service attachment, warranty, uptime, delivery, repeat business, and references. Publicly available comparable cross-company satisfaction data is limited; Yaskawa’s 4.7/5.0 is a single-company, self-reported signal.[8](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/yaskawa-motoman-exceeds-expectations-for-annual-customer-survey-reflecting-the-companys-strong-commitment-to-customer-satisfaction)

## Caveats

- **Cyclicality:** IFR reports installations down in several major regions in 2024, and ABB reported 2023 orders down 26% even as revenue rose from backlog and pricing.[1](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years) [6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1091587/000110465924026633/abb20231231.htm)
- **Forecasts are not outcomes:** IFR’s 2025–28 outlook is an industry forecast and should be stress-tested against macroeconomics, trade restrictions, semiconductor cycles, and China demand.[1](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years)
- **Company scope differs:** ABB, Rockwell, Siemens, Schneider, and Honeywell are diversified; robotics may be only one business area. Reported company margins are not pure-play robotics margins.
- **Customer evidence is thin and nonstandardized:** satisfaction scores are usually vendor-sponsored; installed base and service growth are useful proxies but not direct measures of customer sentiment.
- **Census robotics data is experimental:** the Census Bureau warns that the product may not meet all statistical-quality standards.[2](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2024/econ/2021-asm-robotic-equipment.html)
