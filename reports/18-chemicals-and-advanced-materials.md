# Chemicals and advanced materials

## Bottom line for the 20/20 data refinery

**Sector thesis (measured evidence plus clearly labeled judgment):** Chemicals and advanced materials are financially attractive in the 2020s because they sit upstream of almost every manufactured product, combine large recurring base demand with high-value performance formulations, and are being pulled by semiconductor fabrication, electrification, batteries, energy transition, healthcare, and infrastructure. The strongest economics are not uniform across commodity chemicals: specialty formulations, electronic materials, industrial gases, engineered polymers, coatings, catalysts, and process technologies can have qualification barriers, technical switching costs, and pricing power. Commodity exposure remains cyclical and energy/feedstock intensive.

The U.S. Department of Commerce says chemicals represented roughly **20% of U.S. manufacturing GDP in 2024**, invested **$14.8 billion in R&D**, and generated **$323 billion of exports** (about 16% of total U.S. exports). It also identifies more than 280,000 downstream-cluster and 190,000 upstream-cluster jobs. These are sector-scale indicators, not proof that every company is profitable. [1](https://www.trade.gov/selectusa-chemicals-industry)

CISA's chemical-sector profile describes more than **70,000 products**, more than **750,000 end users**, 529,000 direct U.S. jobs and over 4.1 million jobs supported in the 2022 profile. It reports that 96% of U.S. goods are manufactured using chemical-sector products and identifies agricultural, basic, specialty, and consumer products as the four major components. These figures demonstrate embeddedness and breadth; they should not be interpreted as current 2024 estimates because the profile is dated March 2022. [2](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-02/chemical_sector_profile_final_508_2022_0.pdf)

## Why the sector can be lucrative in the 2020s

1. **Essential, broad-based demand.** Chemistry is a production input into packaging, construction, transportation, food, pharmaceuticals, agriculture, electronics, water treatment, and consumer goods. This breadth can cushion a company when one end market weakens, although it does not remove cyclicality.

2. **High-growth technology pull.** Semiconductor manufacturing requires ultra-high-purity gases, deposition/etch materials, photoresists, encapsulants, coatings, and specialty process chemicals. Air Liquide reported 2024 Electronics revenue growth of **3.3%**, with margin growth supported by higher volumes, particularly advanced materials and carrier gases. [5](https://www.airliquide.com/sites/airliquide.com/files/2025-02/air-liquide-pr-fy-2024-building-on-record-margin-improvement-and-major-commercial-successes-fueling-future-growth-air-liquide-is-once-again-raising-its-margin-ambition.pdf)

3. **Electrification and battery materials.** The IEA reports global EV-battery demand exceeded **750 GWh in 2023**, up 40% year over year. That supports demand for cathode/anode materials, electrolytes, separators, binders, thermal-management materials, conductive additives, specialty coatings, and recycling technologies. Battery demand is a demand signal, not a guarantee of supplier margins; chemistry shifts, oversupply, and raw-material prices can pressure returns. [10](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2024/trends-in-electric-vehicle-batteries)

4. **Energy transition and efficiency.** Lightweight composites, insulation, membranes, catalysts, corrosion protection, low-carbon cement additives, solar encapsulants, and hydrogen-related materials can gain from decarbonization investment. Dow's 2024 filing explicitly describes customer demand for higher-performing, more circular, and more sustainable materials, and lists solar-PV encapsulants, aerospace composites, electronics, mobility, and infrastructure applications. [4](https://s23.q4cdn.com/981382065/files/doc_financials/2024/ar/Dow_2024_Annual_Report_Web.pdf)

5. **Qualification, reliability, and switching costs.** In electronics, aerospace, medical, automotive, and industrial applications, customers qualify materials against strict performance and reliability specifications. This is a reason to expect stronger economics in some specialty niches than in undifferentiated bulk chemicals. It is a sector judgment, not a measured ranking; customer evidence below shows the attributes buyers prioritize.

6. **Pricing and productivity can protect margins.** Air Liquide reported 2024 Gas & Services revenue of €25.81 billion, recurring operating income of €5.555 billion and a 21.5% operating margin, with a 4.0% Industrial Merchant price effect and a 100-basis-point margin improvement excluding energy impact. This is evidence of pricing and efficiency in a particular business model, not a sector average. [5](https://www.airliquide.com/sites/airliquide.com/files/2025-02/air-liquide-pr-fy-2024-building-on-record-margin-improvement-and-major-commercial-successes-fueling-future-growth-air-liquide-is-once-again-raising-its-margin-ambition.pdf)

7. **Scale and R&D create defensibility.** BASF reported 2024 sales of €65.3 billion, EBITDA before special items of €7.9 billion, R&D expense of €2.1 billion, and €6.0 billion of capital expenditure. Its diversified Chemicals, Materials, Industrial Solutions, and Nutrition & Care portfolio illustrates the scale required for process, application, and sustainability R&D. BASF's reported EBITDA-before-special-items margin was approximately 12.1% (calculated from the disclosed figures), but this is a company metric rather than a sector benchmark. [3](https://report.basf.com/2024/en/overviews/2024-at-a-glance.html)

## Most relevant industries / end markets

- **Semiconductors and advanced electronics:** high-purity gases, electronic chemicals, photoresists, dielectric and packaging materials, thermal materials, specialty coatings, and display materials.
- **Batteries, electric vehicles, and mobility:** cathode/anode active materials, electrolytes, separators, binders, conductive additives, lightweight polymers and composites, adhesives, thermal management, and recycling.
- **Renewable power and hydrogen:** solar encapsulants and backsheet materials, wind-turbine composites and coatings, electrolyzers, membranes, catalysts, storage materials, and hydrogen-compatible infrastructure.
- **Construction and infrastructure:** insulation, sealants, coatings, concrete admixtures, pipes, membranes, corrosion protection, engineered plastics, and asphalt modifiers.
- **Packaging, food, and consumer products:** polymer resins, barrier films, adhesives, coatings, surfactants, additives, personal-care ingredients, and food ingredients.
- **Automotive and aerospace:** composites, high-performance polymers, adhesives, coatings, lubricants, flame retardants, lightweighting materials, and cabin/thermal materials.
- **Healthcare and life sciences:** medical gases, pharmaceutical ingredients and excipients, diagnostic materials, sterilization chemicals, membranes, and biomaterials.
- **Agriculture and water:** fertilizers, crop-protection chemistry, water-treatment chemicals, membranes, ion exchange, and process aids.
- **Industrial gases and process services:** oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, argon, specialty gases, and on-site supply systems for refining, metals, healthcare, food, and electronics.

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals

B2B chemicals rarely have a comparable public satisfaction index across suppliers. The most decision-useful free signals are therefore customer surveys, supplier awards, retention/renewal disclosures where reported, complaint and delivery metrics, quality certifications, and evidence of repeat qualification or long-term contracts.

- **Large cross-industry B2B benchmark:** Dow's 2024 AMCX Consortium survey included 16 large B2B companies, including nine chemical manufacturers, three chemical distributors, and more than 7,200 customer responses from over 2,000 executives and 3,000 procurement personas. The benchmark measured NPS, customer satisfaction, digital experience, sustainability, trust, product quality, delivery reliability, service, billing, and account relationships. Across sectors, trust, product/solution quality, and order-delivery reliability were the three most important factors; delivery reliability, service/support handling, digital self-serve, and ease of working together had the lowest satisfaction scores. NPS benchmarks ranged from 7 to 44. Only the stated range and qualitative findings are public; the other quantitative results are consortium-only. [14](https://corporate.dow.com/content/dam/corp/documents/about/824-00102-01-amcx-consortium-2024-survey-results.pdf)
- **Direct semiconductor-customer supplier signal:** Applied Materials' 2024 Supplier Excellence Awards evaluated technical and operational performance including quality, service, lead time, delivery, cost, responsiveness, ESG, aftermarket support, and innovation/new-product support. Sixteen suppliers received awards, including a specific Excellence in Quality category. This is a customer-recognized performance signal, not an independent satisfaction score and not evidence that all suppliers or winners rank overall. [15](https://ir.appliedmaterials.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-materials-announces-supplier-excellence-awards)
- **What to collect for a 20/20 company screen:** disclosed NPS/CSAT, on-time-in-full delivery, complaint rate and response time, product-return/quality incidents, warranty or recall rates, renewal/expansion, customer concentration and tenure, qualification wins, supplier awards from major OEMs, ISO/IATF/AS certification scope, and price-versus-volume/mix evidence. Treat company-reported measures as directional unless independently audited.

## Financial and operating metrics available from free public data

- Revenue, organic growth, segment sales, volume/price/mix, EBITDA/EBIT, operating margin, ROIC/ROCE, free cash flow, capex, R&D, and net debt from annual reports and SEC filings.
- End-market and geographic exposure, capacity additions, utilization, backlog, project start-ups, and product/application descriptions from annual reports and investor presentations.
- Commodity/feedstock exposure: oil, gas, naphtha, electricity, ethylene, propylene, chlorine, ammonia, lithium, nickel, cobalt, graphite and rare-earth prices from government, exchange, IEA, USGS and company disclosures.
- Innovation and defensibility: R&D spending, patents where disclosed, new-product launches, qualification wins, customer co-development, and technical service footprint.
- Customer quality: NPS/CSAT, supplier awards, delivery reliability, quality incidents, complaint handling, certifications, contract duration, retention/renewal, and customer concentration.
- Sustainability/regulatory: Scope 1/2 emissions, energy intensity, recycled content, product carbon footprint, hazardous-substance compliance, remediation liabilities, and environmental fines.
- Public market cross-checks: share price, market capitalization, enterprise value, dividends, short interest and consensus estimates from free exchange/company/SEC sources; do not mix fiscal periods or GAAP and non-GAAP definitions.

## Caveats

- **No sector-wide profitability ranking is asserted.** Chemicals spans low-margin, cyclical bulk products and high-margin specialty/technology niches.
- BASF, Dow and Air Liquide figures are company-reported and use different currencies, segment definitions and non-GAAP measures; they are examples, not directly comparable peer rankings.
- U.S. government statistics use different scopes and years: the SelectUSA page reports 2024 data, while the CISA profile largely reports 2020 or earlier data and should be dated as such.
- Battery, semiconductor and energy-transition growth can coexist with poor supplier returns because of capacity oversupply, rapid technology substitution, raw-material volatility, high capex, geopolitics, export controls and customer concentration.
- Customer-quality data are sparse and often proprietary. Dow's AMCX results provide a useful public benchmark but are not a probability sample of the whole sector; Applied Materials' awards are a customer recognition program rather than an independent study.
- Environmental, health, safety and regulatory liabilities can materially affect valuation and cash flow, especially for legacy assets and hazardous materials.
