# 20/20 Data Refinery — Real Estate and Construction

## Sector assessment

**Sector:** Real estate and construction (U.S.-anchored evidence, with global commercial real estate where company filings report it). The sector includes ownership and operation of property; real-estate services and financing; homebuilding and multifamily development; commercial, industrial, infrastructure and specialty construction; building materials and home-improvement distribution.

**Why it can be financially lucrative in the 2020s.** The opportunity is not uniform across the sector, but several durable mechanisms support attractive revenue and profit pools:

1. **Large, essential spending base.** The U.S. Census Bureau’s construction-spending program measures private residential, private nonresidential and public construction. Its current release reported a $2.203 trillion seasonally adjusted annual rate for total construction in August 2026, including $882.3 billion residential and $773.0 billion private nonresidential spending ([Census construction spending](https://www.census.gov/construction/c30/current/index.html)). This is a very large addressable market; reported figures are spending, not industry profit.
2. **Structural housing undersupply and replacement demand.** Census tracks permits, starts, units under construction and completions for privately owned housing ([Census New Residential Construction](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/)). NAHB/HBI reported in October 2024 that the U.S. housing deficit was estimated at 1.5 million homes and that the industry needed approximately 723,000 construction-worker hires per year; the same report cited 8.3 million payroll construction workers, including 3.4 million in residential construction ([NAHB/HBI labor report](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2024/10/hbi-construction-labor-report-fall-2024)). The deficit supports long-run demand, while labor scarcity can also support pricing for scarce skilled capacity. The 1.5 million figure is an industry estimate, not a Census count.
3. **Logistics, e-commerce and data-center/mission-critical demand.** Prologis’ 2024 Form 10-K says its operating portfolio was 95.8% occupied at December 31, 2024 and rent change on leases commencing during 2024 was 68.7% on a net-effective basis. It also reported a roughly 30% lease mark-to-market and $36.9 billion of potential development value in its consolidated land and other real-estate investments ([Prologis 2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045609/000095017025021272/pld-20241231.htm)). These are company-specific operating metrics, not proof that every industrial landlord earns similar returns, but they illustrate why well-located logistics assets can be lucrative.
4. **Recurring, fee-based services can be more resilient than transactions.** CBRE’s 2024 Form 10-K describes recurring facilities management, project management, property management, valuation, loan servicing and investment-management fees as resilient business lines. CBRE reported 2024 revenue of $35.8 billion, net revenue of $20.9 billion and core EBITDA of $2.7 billion; net revenue from resilient business lines was $14.082 billion ([CBRE 2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1138118/000113811825000005/cbre-20241231.htm)). This supports a judgment that outsourced operations, compliance, maintenance and asset-management services may compound more steadily than brokerage alone.
5. **Homebuilder operating leverage and land/production scale.** D.R. Horton’s fiscal 2024 annual report recorded $36.801 billion revenue, $4.756 billion net income, 89,690 homebuilding closings and $33.904 billion of home-sales revenue. Home-sales gross profit was $8.0 billion, or 23.5% of home-sales revenue; pre-tax income was $6.285 billion, or 17.1% of total revenue ([D.R. Horton 2024 annual report](https://investor.drhorton.com/~/media/Files/D/D-R-Horton-IR/documents/2024-dhi-annual-report.pdf)). These results show that scaled builders can earn substantial profits, while the company also disclosed elevated incentives and affordability pressure. They should not be generalized into a sector-wide margin claim.

**Main counterforces:** mortgage rates and credit availability; property valuations and capitalization rates; construction wages, materials and insurance; permitting and zoning; cyclicality in transaction volumes; environmental/climate exposure; leverage/refinancing; customer concentration; and execution/warranty risk. Real estate is asset-intensive, and high nominal revenue does not automatically mean high free cash flow or high return on invested capital.

## Most relevant industries / subsectors

- **Residential homebuilding:** single-family and multifamily development, build-to-rent, manufactured housing and land development.
- **Commercial and industrial property ownership:** logistics/warehouses, data centers, life-science/laboratory, retail, multifamily, office and specialty facilities.
- **Real-estate investment vehicles:** REITs, private real-estate funds, mortgage REITs and asset managers.
- **Brokerage, leasing, valuation and capital-markets services:** property sales, leasing, mortgage origination, valuation and advisory.
- **Property, facilities and project management:** recurring outsourced services for owners and large occupiers.
- **General and specialty contractors:** commercial building, civil/infrastructure, industrial, electrical/mechanical, environmental and restoration work.
- **Building products and distribution:** cement/aggregates, engineered products, HVAC/plumbing/electrical, roofing, insulation and home-improvement retail.
- **Construction technology and services:** estimating, design/build software, project controls, building inspection, energy efficiency and prefabrication.

This is a relevance-based industry map, not a ranking by profitability.

## Candidate leading companies

The following are **candidates for screening**, selected because their public filings, scale or category position make them useful starting points. This is not an invented ranking.

- **Prologis** — global logistics/industrial real-estate owner and developer; 2024 filing provides occupancy, rent-change and development-pipeline evidence.
- **CBRE Group** — commercial real-estate services, facilities/project management, valuation, capital markets and investment management; its filing identifies it as the largest commercial real-estate services and investment firm by 2024 revenue.
- **D.R. Horton** — scaled U.S. homebuilder with mortgage/title and rental operations; fiscal 2024 report provides detailed volume, profitability and balance-sheet data.
- **Lennar** — major U.S. homebuilder and mortgage/title provider; candidate for comparison with D.R. Horton using SEC filings and annual reports.
- **NVR** — U.S. homebuilder and mortgage-banking operator with an asset-light land strategy; candidate for margin, returns and cycle comparison.
- **JLL** and **Cushman & Wakefield** — commercial real-estate services and facilities/project-management peers to CBRE.
- **Public Storage**, **Equinix**, **American Tower** and **Ventas** — specialty/property REIT candidates with different demand drivers (self-storage, data centers, communications infrastructure and healthcare real estate).
- **Jacobs**, **Quanta Services**, **EMCOR** and **AECOM** — engineering, infrastructure, utility and specialty-contractor candidates.
- **Builders FirstSource**, **CRH**, **Vulcan Materials**, **Caterpillar**, **Home Depot** and **Lowe’s** — building-products, aggregates/equipment and distribution candidates.

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals

Customer satisfaction is best treated as a **leading or diagnostic signal**, not as proof of financial performance. Public measures are concentrated in mortgage, home-improvement retail and selected builder studies; they do not cover all construction contractors or landlords.

- **Mortgage origination:** J.D. Power’s 2024 U.S. Mortgage Origination Satisfaction Study scored overall satisfaction at **727/1,000**, down 3 points year over year. It found that satisfaction was 40 points higher when local brand representatives were directly involved, 41 points higher when lenders engaged customers early, and that trust satisfaction was 133 points higher among borrowers who strongly relied on lender expertise. Prosperity Home Mortgage ranked highest at 772, followed by Movement Mortgage at 761 and Bank of America at 760. The study covered 7,534 customers and six factors: communication, digital channels, trust, needs-fit, ease of doing business and people ([J.D. Power 2024 mortgage origination study](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2024-us-mortgage-origination-satisfaction-study/)).
- **Home-improvement retail:** J.D. Power’s 2025 study reported that 64% of shoppers would definitely shop at their store again, up 9 percentage points from 2024. Lowe’s scored highest at 680 and Ace Hardware second at 672. The 2,143-customer study measured additional services, digital tools, trust, people, products/supplies, returns, store/facility and value ([J.D. Power 2025 home-improvement retailer study](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2025-us-home-improvement-retailer-satisfaction-study/)). Repeat-intent and dimension scores are useful customer-quality signals for distribution, but they are not a measure of contractor or builder quality.
- **Operational/customer proxies for real estate:** For landlords, relevant observable signals include occupancy, renewal rate, lease retention, tenant concentration, rent collection, complaint/service response, building certifications and tenant survey results. Prologis’ 95.8% occupancy and 68.7% 2024 rent change are operating indicators, but neither is a direct tenant-satisfaction score.
- **Builder quality proxies:** public screens should include warranty claims, construction-defect litigation, cancellation rates, customer-recommendation or referral rates, cycle time, delivered-vs-promised dates, post-close service response and independent new-home satisfaction studies when available. Avoid treating online reviews as representative without a defined sampling method.

## Suggested 20/20 data fields

- **Demand:** permits, starts, completions, units under construction, vacancy, absorption, rents, same-store NOI, transaction volume, backlog and book-to-bill.
- **Economics:** revenue, gross margin, EBITDA/EBIT margin, net income, operating cash flow, free cash flow, ROIC/ROE, cap rate, debt maturity, interest coverage, leverage and land inventory.
- **Operating quality:** occupancy, lease renewal/retention, rent collection, construction cycle time, backlog conversion, change orders, rework, warranty claims, safety rates and defect litigation.
- **Customer:** satisfaction score, recommendation/advocacy, repeat purchase, complaint rate, response time, digital experience, trust, needs-fit and value-for-price.
- **Workforce/supply:** skilled-labor availability, wage inflation, material-price indices, subcontractor concentration and permitting duration.

## Caveats and interpretation

- Evidence here is primarily U.S. and mostly 2024–2026 public data; local housing, rate and regulatory conditions can differ sharply.
- Company filings are authoritative for the named company but contain management definitions and selective metrics. Do not compare adjusted EBITDA, core EPS, NOI or net revenue without reconciling definitions.
- “Leading” means a candidate with scale, public disclosure or category relevance. No overall company ranking is asserted.
- Construction spending and home-sales revenue are activity measures, not profit measures. Profitability depends on project mix, financing, land basis, pricing, execution and cycle timing.
- Customer studies often cover a specific journey and sample. Mortgage-originator and home-improvement-retailer results cannot be used as a direct ranking of builders, landlords or contractors.
- NAHB/HBI’s housing-deficit and labor estimates are industry analysis. They should be cross-checked against Census, HUD and BLS series before use in a quantitative model.
