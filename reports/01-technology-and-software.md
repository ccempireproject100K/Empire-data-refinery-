# 20/20 Data Refinery — Technology and software

## Bottom line: why the sector is financially lucrative in the 2020s

Technology and software is financially attractive because digital products can be replicated at low marginal cost, sold globally, and increasingly delivered through recurring subscriptions or usage-based cloud contracts. Those characteristics can produce high gross margins, predictable renewal revenue, strong operating leverage, and substantial cash generation once a product reaches scale. The economics are not uniform: data-center, semiconductor, telecom, implementation, and cybersecurity costs can be capital-intensive, and AI infrastructure is currently pressuring cloud gross margins.

There is direct evidence of demand expansion. Gartner forecast worldwide public-cloud end-user spending of **$675.4 billion in 2024**, up **20.4%** from 2023; SaaS was the largest category at **$247.2 billion**, up 20.0%, while IaaS was forecast to grow 25.6%. Gartner attributes the growth to generative AI and application modernization ([Gartner, 2024](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-05-20-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-public-cloud-end-user-spending-to-surpass-675-billion-in-2024)). This is a forecast, not realized revenue, but it is a useful external demand indicator.

Official U.S. data also show sustained expansion in software publishing. U.S. Census data reported through the Federal Reserve’s FRED show software-publisher revenue of **$350.1 billion in 2020**, **$402.4 billion in 2021**, and **$453.5 billion in 2022**—a 29.5% increase from 2020 to 2022 ([FRED/U.S. Census, REVEF5112ALLEST](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVEF5112ALLEST)). This series is annual and revised; it is not a forecast and covers U.S. software publishers rather than the entire global technology sector.

Scale economics are visible in leading-company filings. Microsoft reported FY2025 revenue of **$281.7 billion**, up 15%, operating income of **$128.5 billion**, up 17%, and Azure revenue above **$75 billion**, up 34%. Microsoft Cloud revenue was **$168.9 billion**; its cloud gross margin was 69%, with Microsoft explicitly noting that AI infrastructure investment reduced the percentage ([Microsoft FY2025 Annual Report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html)). Salesforce reported FY2025 revenue of **$37.9 billion**, subscription-and-support revenue of **$35.7 billion** (about 94% of revenue), GAAP operating margin of 19.0%, operating cash flow of $13.1 billion, and total remaining performance obligations of $63.4 billion ([Salesforce FY2025 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2025/Salesforce-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx); [Salesforce FY2025 SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1108524/000110852425000006/crm-20250131.htm)). These are measured company results, not proof that every software company is profitable.

Adobe’s FY2024 filing describes a product-led subscription model designed to increase adoption, usage, conversion, expansion, loyalty, and predictable recurring revenue. It also identifies generative AI features as a mechanism to raise product value, engagement, and retention ([Adobe FY2024 Annual Report](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/adbe-2024-annual-report.pdf)). The favorable model therefore rests on **recurrence plus switching costs**, not merely on technology novelty.

## Most relevant industries and subsectors

1. **Cloud infrastructure and platforms (IaaS/PaaS):** Compute, storage, databases, networking, developer tools, and AI model training/inference. Growth is supported by modernization and AI workloads, but margins depend on power, chips, data centers, and utilization.
2. **Enterprise SaaS and workflow applications:** CRM, ERP, HCM, collaboration, productivity, marketing, finance, and vertical applications. Subscription contracts support revenue visibility; integration and data lock-in can lower churn.
3. **Cybersecurity and identity:** Endpoint, cloud, network, application, data, identity, and security operations products. Security is a mission-critical budget category, but competition and rapid threat evolution are intense.
4. **AI software and data/analytics:** Foundation-model access, copilots, agents, model tooling, data platforms, observability, and decision software. The opportunity is large, but pricing, inference costs, copyright/regulatory risk, and customer ROI remain unsettled.
5. **Semiconductors and AI hardware:** Accelerators, CPUs, networking, memory, and semiconductor equipment. AI demand can generate exceptional growth and pricing power, but this is more cyclical and capital-intensive than SaaS.
6. **Digital advertising, search, and consumer platforms:** Monetization of attention, intent, commerce, and data. Scale and network effects are powerful, while privacy, regulation, and platform concentration are material risks.
7. **IT services, systems integration, and managed services:** Migration, implementation, consulting, outsourcing, and support. Demand benefits from complexity, but labor intensity usually limits margins relative to software licenses.
8. **Telecommunications and business connectivity:** Broadband, wireless, fiber, edge, and enterprise connectivity. Customer-quality evidence is comparatively mature, but capital requirements and price competition are high.
9. **Vertical software and fintech infrastructure:** Software specialized for healthcare, education, government, construction, payments, banking, logistics, and other regulated workflows. Domain expertise and embedded processes can support retention.
10. **Gaming and digital media:** Games, content subscriptions, creator tools, and streaming. Recurring engagement is valuable, but hit-driven demand and content costs create volatility.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics places software publishing in NAICS 5112 and explains that it is included in publishing because creating and bringing a copyrighted product to market is analogous to other intellectual-property publishing activities ([BLS Industry at a Glance](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag511.htm)). This definition is narrower than “technology” and should not be used as a complete sector boundary.

## Candidate leading companies (not a ranking)

These are **candidates for analysis**, selected for scale, strategic relevance, disclosed recurring or platform economics, and/or exposure to the subsectors above. The list is judgment, not a measured league table:

- **Microsoft** — Azure, Microsoft 365, Windows, Dynamics, cybersecurity, developer tools, and AI infrastructure.
- **Amazon** — AWS cloud infrastructure, data services, and enterprise technology ecosystem.
- **Alphabet** — search and digital advertising, cloud, AI models, and consumer platforms.
- **NVIDIA** — AI accelerators, networking, and software ecosystem; high growth but semiconductor cyclicality.
- **Apple** — devices, operating systems, services, and a large ecosystem; hardware-heavy economics.
- **Oracle** — database, enterprise applications, and cloud infrastructure.
- **Salesforce** — CRM, data, marketing, service, and AI; unusually high subscription mix.
- **Adobe** — creative, document, and experience software with recurring subscriptions and AI features.
- **ServiceNow** — enterprise workflow and IT service management subscriptions.
- **SAP** — enterprise applications, data, and cloud migration.
- **Intuit** — tax, accounting, personal finance, and small-business software.
- **CrowdStrike** — cloud-delivered cybersecurity and endpoint/security operations.
- **Cisco** — networking, security, collaboration, and infrastructure; technology plus hardware/services.
- **IBM** — hybrid cloud, consulting, infrastructure, and enterprise software.

A sensible 20/20 comparison should separate **platform owners**, **subscription application vendors**, **hardware/chip companies**, **advertising platforms**, and **services firms** rather than treating all of them as directly comparable.

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals

The strongest public customer-quality signals are behavioral and financial rather than generic popularity scores:

- **Renewal, retention, and expansion:** Net retention, dollar-based net retention, logo churn, subscription renewal rates, seat growth, usage growth, and remaining performance obligations. Salesforce’s filing states that subscriptions typically run 12–36 months and that non-renewal, downsell, or customer dissatisfaction can reduce future revenue ([Salesforce SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1108524/000110852425000006/crm-20250131.htm)). Adobe similarly identifies renewal rates, attrition, satisfaction, reliability, security, price, and customer activity as drivers of future results ([Adobe FY2024 Annual Report](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/adbe-2024-annual-report.pdf)).
- **Usage and realized value:** Active users, seats, consumption, feature adoption, successful implementations, support resolution, uptime, incident frequency, and time-to-value. Salesforce reported that Agentforce had handled 380,000 help conversations with an 84% resolution rate and 2% human escalation in its FY2025 release; this is a company-reported operational signal, not an independent satisfaction ranking ([Salesforce FY2025 results](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2025/Salesforce-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx)).
- **Independent satisfaction studies:** J.D. Power’s 2025 U.S. Business Internet Satisfaction Study surveyed 4,008 business customers and scored satisfaction on a 1,000-point scale across performance/reliability, cost, communications, sales, billing, digital account management, and customer service. Overall satisfaction rose 12 points to 707; AT&T led large enterprise (743) and medium business (708), while Spectrum Business led small business (656) ([J.D. Power, 2025](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2025-us-business-internet-satisfaction-study/)). This is a measured ranking for business internet, not for software vendors generally.
- **ACSI benchmarks:** The American Customer Satisfaction Index describes a stable, multivariable, cross-industry methodology and links satisfaction to retention and financial outcomes. Its relevant public sectors include AI/interactive media/subscription entertainment and telecommunications ([ACSI](https://theacsi.com/)). ACSI evidence is useful for consumer-facing technology, but coverage and company-level availability vary by category.
- **Public review and complaint signals:** App-store ratings, software review sites, Better Business Bureau/consumer complaints, outage histories, security incidents, and support-response disclosures can supplement filings. These are noisy and selection-biased; they should not be combined into an invented composite ranking.

## What to measure in a 20/20 data refinery

- Revenue growth, organic growth, recurring/subscription share, remaining performance obligations, deferred revenue, bookings, and billings.
- Gross margin, operating margin, free-cash-flow margin, R&D intensity, sales-and-marketing payback, and capital expenditure.
- Net retention, logo retention/churn, renewal rate, average contract value, seat/user growth, usage/consumption growth, and customer concentration.
- Cloud revenue and gross margin, data-center/power/chip costs, AI revenue, AI inference cost, and AI product adoption.
- Uptime, severity and duration of outages, security incidents, vulnerability response, support resolution, implementation duration, and product adoption.
- Independent satisfaction score, survey sample/methodology, recommendation or loyalty measures, app ratings, complaint rates, and review-volume trends.
- Market structure: switching costs, interoperability, open-source substitution, network effects, distribution control, regulation, privacy, antitrust, and intellectual-property exposure.

## Caveats

- “Technology and software” is not a single accounting or statistical industry. The candidate set spans software, cloud, advertising, hardware, semiconductors, telecom, and services.
- Forecasts such as Gartner’s are not realized results. Company-reported metrics are subject to management definitions and may not be comparable across issuers.
- High growth does not guarantee high profitability. AI infrastructure, chip supply, energy, data-center depreciation, cybersecurity costs, stock compensation, and content costs can materially reduce returns.
- Customer satisfaction is not identical to financial quality. A high score may coexist with weak pricing power, and a profitable platform may have mixed user sentiment. Use satisfaction alongside retention, usage, reliability, and cash-flow evidence.
- Do not infer “leading” from this candidate list. The report intentionally avoids an unsupported overall ranking.
