# 20/20 Sector Research Synthesis

**Purpose.** This report turns the supplied 20 sector briefs into a repeatable, evidence-aware screening framework. It is **not investment advice**, does not recommend securities, and does not assert that any company or sector is a definitive winner. The ordering below is a **provisional research-priority ranking**: it indicates where a normalized diligence process should begin, not a comparable, audited league table.

**Research vintage.** The underlying briefs principally use 2023–2025 disclosures and public releases, with a small number of newer source releases (for example, the supplied U.S. e-commerce observation is Q2 2026). Fiscal years, geographies, accounting definitions, and source methodologies differ. All scores, weights, and ranks in this synthesis are **proposed estimates** until a standardized data set is collected.

> **Core conclusion:** The strongest 2020s screening opportunities are where scalable or protected profit pools meet durable demand, recurring/contracted revenue, high switching costs or network effects, and enough public evidence to test the thesis. Technology/software, AI infrastructure, healthcare, finance, energy, defense, commerce platforms, and industrial automation qualify for early diligence—but each contains materially different business models and risks.

---

## 1. How to use this report

1. **Use sectors as a starting universe, not as peer groups.** A cloud platform, a chip designer, a regulated utility, and a biotech developer should not be compared on one unadjusted margin or satisfaction metric.
2. **Split every sector into its economic sub-industries first.** Then compare companies only to close operating peers and normalize the fiscal period, currency, accounting method, and capital intensity.
3. **Separate measured facts from analyst judgment.** The supplied research contains official statistics, filings, independent surveys, forecasts, and issuer-defined KPIs. These should receive different evidentiary weight.
4. **Score the sector and the company separately.** This report ranks sector research priority; it does not convert candidate-company lists into stock picks.
5. **Require a customer-quality check.** Growth and margin alone are insufficient. Test retention, reliability, complaints, service, safety, implementation time, and independent customer evidence where available.

### Decision gates before any company conclusion

| Gate | Minimum question | Examples of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Economic model | Where is the recurring or protected profit pool, and what capital is required to sustain it? | Subscription/renewal revenue, payment take rate, regulated rate base, patents, backlog, installed base, cash conversion |
| Financial quality | Is profitability durable after normalizing cycles and issuer adjustments? | GAAP operating margin, FCF/operating cash flow, ROIC/ROE where meaningful, leverage, working capital |
| Growth quality | Is growth volume-led and repeatable rather than price, acquisition, subsidy, or a one-off? | Organic growth, cohort/retention, RPO, bookings, utilization, capacity, real versus nominal growth |
| Customer quality | Do customers stay, get value, and receive reliable service? | Independent satisfaction, NPS/CSAT methodology, churn, uptime, complaints normalized by accounts, safety/quality outcomes |
| Evidence quality | Can a third party reproduce the claim with comparable, dated data? | Official data, audited filings, transparent survey samples, reconciliations, definitions, consistent time series |
| Risk and valuation | Does the return compensate for regulation, disruption, cyclicality, concentration, and price? | Scenario analysis, regulatory review, sensitivity to demand/price/rates/input costs; valuation is outside this sector-only ranking |

## 2. Recommended ranking methodology

### 2.1 Unit of analysis and comparability rules

**Tier 1 — sector screen.** Score each of the 20 sectors after splitting into homogeneous sub-industries. The provisional table in Section 3 is this tier.

**Tier 2 — sub-industry score.** For example, split “technology and software” into cloud/IaaS, enterprise SaaS, cybersecurity, semiconductors, digital advertising, and IT services. Do not blend their revenue recognition, gross-margin structure, or capex.

**Tier 3 — company score.** Score each candidate only against its closest peer group. A company with several segments needs segment-level scoring and a transparent sum-of-parts view.

**Normalization requirements**

- Use the latest completed annual period plus LTM, label both, and translate non-calendar fiscal years explicitly.
- Use reported GAAP/IFRS measures first. Present adjusted metrics only alongside their reconciliation and with the issuer’s definition.
- Convert currency at a stated average-period rate for income-statement flows and closing rate for balance-sheet data; retain local-currency growth.
- Compare regulated utilities using rate base, allowed ROE, leverage, reliability, and regulatory outcomes; compare banks using capital, credit, NIM, efficiency, and returns; compare pre-profit biotech using probability-adjusted pipeline economics rather than current operating margin.
- Treat GMV, bookings, payment volume, backlog, installed capacity, and subscribers as **operating indicators**, not revenue or profit.
- Use independent customer metrics only within the same study, geography, period, and respondent base. Missing customer data is “not available,” not a neutral or positive score.

### 2.2 Proposed five-pillar score

Each pillar is rated **1.0–5.0**, using a published rubric. The composite is a proposed screening score—not a measure of expected return.

\[
\textbf{Sector Priority Score} = 0.35P + 0.25O + 0.20G + 0.10C + 0.10E
\]

Where:

| Symbol | Pillar | Proposed weight | What it measures | Illustrative inputs |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **P** | Profitability and cash economics | 35% | Magnitude, resilience, and cash conversion of the relevant profit pool | GAAP operating margin, FCF margin, ROIC/ROE, efficiency ratio, gross margin, leverage, capital intensity, cash conversion |
| **O** | Lucrativeness / opportunity | 25% | Addressable demand, structural pricing/return potential, and barriers to capture | Demand scale, secular drivers, recurring/contracted revenue, regulation, IP, network effects, switching costs, installed base |
| **G** | Growth quality | 20% | Growth durability and quality rather than headline growth alone | Organic revenue/volume growth, RPO/backlog, retention, adoption, utilization, capacity, market growth; adjusted for acquisitions/subsidies/inflation |
| **C** | Customer quality | 10% | Customer experience, trust, reliability, and retention evidence | ACSI/J.D. Power/CAHPS where comparable, renewal/churn, complaints per account, uptime, delivery/on-time, recalls, safety |
| **E** | Evidence quality and comparability | 10% | Reproducibility, coverage, timeliness, and definition quality | Official statistics, audited filings, transparent methodology, reconciliations, consistent time series, breadth of coverage |

**Illustrative rating anchors**

| Rating | Profitability / opportunity / growth interpretation | Customer / evidence interpretation |
|---:|---|---|
| 5 | Clearly strong and durable for relevant peers, supported by multiple standardized sources | Independent, current, transparent, comparable data with broad coverage |
| 4 | Attractive with good corroboration; risks do not dominate | Solid, though incomplete, independent/customer evidence and well-defined filings |
| 3 | Mixed, cyclical, or materially heterogeneous | Partial, dated, issuer-reported, or geographically limited evidence |
| 2 | Weak economics or limited scalability without a credible offset | Sparse, non-comparable, self-selected, or poorly reconciled data |
| 1 | Adverse/unsupported after normalization | No credible or usable evidence |

### 2.3 Confidence and sensitivity overlays

Do **not** hide uncertainty inside one score. Publish these overlays next to each composite:

\[
\textbf{Confidence} = 0.40A + 0.25T + 0.20M + 0.15R
\]

- **A (authority):** share of decision-critical evidence from government/regulators/audited filings.
- **T (timeliness):** recency and update frequency of the evidence.
- **M (method comparability):** consistent definitions, scope, currency, and fiscal period.
- **R (replicability):** URLs/data tables, formulas, reconciliation, and ability to reproduce the calculation.

All four inputs are 1–5; publish the confidence rating separately. Suggested labels: **High** (≥4.0), **Medium** (3.0–3.9), **Low** (<3.0).

**Sensitivity rule.** Recalculate with weights of P 30–40%, O 20–30%, G 15–25%, C 5–15%, and E 5–15%. If an item moves more than three positions or crosses a decision threshold, label it **weight-sensitive**. A scoring tool should also show equal-weight results.

### 2.4 Proposed company-level extensions

The sector formula is deliberately incomplete for a security-level decision. Add:

\[
\textbf{Company Quality Score} = 0.30P + 0.20O + 0.20G + 0.15C + 0.15E
\]

Then apply independent **risk** and **valuation** gates rather than burying them in the score:

- **Risk gate:** credit/liquidity, regulatory, customer/supplier concentration, safety, cybersecurity, litigation, disruption, commodity/rate/FX sensitivity, and execution.
- **Valuation gate:** compare the normalized earnings/cash-flow base and growth/risk profile to market value. Valuation inputs were not supplied in the sector briefs, so no valuation ranking is made here.

### Explicit ranking caveats

1. **This is not a definitive ranking.** It uses proposed weights and estimated rubric ratings because a common, normalized panel was not supplied.
2. **Small score differences are not meaningful.** Treat scores within **0.15** as a priority band and test weight sensitivity.
3. **Sector labels overlap.** Semiconductors sit within AI/data infrastructure and technology; energy infrastructure appears in utilities, renewables, and materials; Amazon and Microsoft span several sectors.
4. **Evidence quality is uneven by design.** Finance, utilities, defense, and public healthcare have strong official data; B2B customer satisfaction, private companies, global marketplace economics, and early-stage biotech are less comparable.
5. **Profitability differs radically inside each label.** Pre-revenue drug discovery, merchant renewables, loss-making EV charging, and commodity freight cannot be represented by a leader’s margin.
6. **Forecasts are not realized results.** Gartner, IEA, IRENA, PwC, OECD-FAO, and industry-association outlooks must be labeled as forecasts/scenarios.
7. **Customer satisfaction is not financial quality.** It is one diagnostic alongside retention, usage, reliability, outcomes, cash flow, and risk.

## 5. Free-source data collection plan

### 5.1 Collection architecture

Build a versioned, auditable data set rather than copying headline claims into a scorecard.

| Workstream | Data to capture | Preferred free sources | Collection procedure | Refresh |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market size and demand | Official sector output/spend, units, installed base, spending, employment, price/volume, forecasts separately labeled | Census, BEA, BLS, CMS, EIA, DOE, FDA, USDA, FCC, OECD, IEA, IRENA, SIPRI, IFR | Save URL, release date, table title/series ID, units, geography, vintage, and whether actual/forecast | Monthly/quarterly as released; annual benchmark refresh |
| Financial statements | Revenue, segment sales, GAAP operating income, net income, cash flow, capex, balance sheet, shares, debt | , issuer annual reports/10-K/20-F | Use audited annual filings first; extract source page/table; retain reported and calculated fields separately | Quarterly for issuers; annual QA after 10-K/20-F |
| Operating KPIs | RPO/backlog, subscriptions, customers, retention, transactions, ARPU, utilization, capacity, reliability | Earnings releases, investor presentations, annual reports; regulators where available | Preserve the issuer definition verbatim; tag **issuer-reported**; do not impute missing values | Quarterly/annual |
| Customer quality | Satisfaction/experience, complaints, service, outcome, retention, reliability | ACSI, J.D. Power, CAHPS/HCAHPS, CMS, CFPB, FAA/FRA, regulators, app stores as supplemental only | Record study universe, sample, dates, scale, methodology, segment and score; compute complaints per relevant denominator | On study release; quarterly for operational complaints |
| Regulatory/safety | Recalls, compliance, clinical approvals, safety outcomes, audits, outages, rate cases | FDA, CMS, FAA, GAO, CISA, state utility commissions, CFPB, FERC/EIA | Time-stamp events and distinguish allegation, finding, enforcement, and resolved action | Monthly monitoring; material-event alerts |
| Forecasts/scenarios | Industry projections, demand, capex, electricity, employment | IEA, IRENA, Gartner, OECD-FAO, BLS, PwC, trade associations | Keep separate from actuals; preserve assumptions, scenario, publication date, forecast horizon | At each release |

### 5.2 Minimum data dictionary

Every observation should include at least:

`entity_id, sector, sub_industry, metric_name, metric_definition, value, unit, currency, period_start, period_end, fiscal_or_calendar, geography, source_type, source_publisher, source_url, source_page_or_table, publication_date, retrieval_date, actual_or_forecast, gaap_if_applicable, adjustment_status, calculation_formula, denominator, confidence_flag, notes`.

**Non-negotiable labels**

- **Actual / estimate / forecast / scenario / target**
- **GAAP/IFRS / adjusted / issuer KPI / independently surveyed / regulator-reported**
- **Fiscal versus calendar period**
- **Gross versus net presentation** (especially marketplace GMV/revenue and merchant processing)
- **Current versus constant currency**
- **Consolidated versus segment**

### 5.3 Metric formulas and guardrails

| Metric | Proposed formula | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | \((Revenue_t / Revenue_{t-1}) - 1\) | Show organic, FX-neutral, acquisition-adjusted, and reported only if disclosed; never invent adjustments |
| FCF margin | \(FCF / Revenue\) | State issuer or analyst FCF definition; reconcile capex treatment and lease impacts |
| Operating margin | \(GAAP\ operating\ income / Revenue\) | Compare comparable segment/consolidated levels only |
| ROIC (when meaningful) | \(NOPAT / average\ invested\ capital\) | Use a documented tax rate and capital definition; not appropriate as a primary metric for banks/insurers or early-stage biotech |
| Net retention | \((starting\ cohort\ revenue + expansion - contraction - churn) / starting\ cohort\ revenue\) | Use issuer definition; report customer eligibility, dollar versus logo basis, and period |
| Churn | \(lost\ customers\ or\ revenue / beginning\ customers\ or\ revenue\) | Do not compare logo churn to revenue churn without a clear label |
| Complaint rate | \(validated\ complaints / active\ customers\ or\ accounts\) × 10,000 | Complaint databases are not representative samples; disclose scope and denominator |
| Reliability | \(1 - downtime / total\ scheduled\ time\)\) or regulator-defined SAIDI/SAIFI | Do not equate a vendor SLA with observed availability; use regulator standards where possible |
| Customer score normalization | \((score - scale\ minimum)/(scale\ maximum-scale\ minimum)\) | Only normalize across same study/category/time; retain raw score and methodology |
| Evidence score | Weighted rating of authority, timeliness, method comparability, replicability | Do not award a high score merely because an issuer publishes a KPI |

### 5.4 Data-quality controls

1. **Source hierarchy:** regulator/official statistics → audited filing → third-party methodology-transparent survey → issuer earnings release → investor presentation → industry association/consultancy forecast → reviews/news/social content.
2. **Dual-source important claims:** validate central market claims through two sources where feasible; retain contradictions rather than averaging them away.
3. **Reconciliation:** maintain links to GAAP-to-adjusted tables; do not rank on “adjusted EBITDA” alone.
4. **Period alignment:** no TTM versus prior fiscal year comparison without a label; align quarters before calculating growth.
5. **Missingness:** display `N/A`, reason, and expected source; never convert non-disclosure into a zero or average value.
6. **Outlier review:** manually review acquisitions/divestitures, impairments, reserves, stock compensation, commodity gains/losses, tax anomalies, and restatements.
7. **Audit trail:** save a PDF/HTML snapshot or quoted table reference for each value and a retrieval date.

### 5.5 Suggested update cadence

| Cadence | Actions |
|---|---|
| **Weekly** | Capture material filings, safety/regulatory actions, major recalls/outages, rate-case decisions, contract awards, and material guidance changes |
| **Quarterly** | Refresh issuer financials and KPIs after earnings; recompute LTM; update liquidity/leverage, churn/retention, backlog, customer complaints, and risk flags |
| **Semiannual** | Refresh independent satisfaction studies, industry dashboards, competitive/market-share checks, and customer review trend diagnostics |
| **Annual** | Rebase sector-size and employment series; review sub-industry taxonomy; refresh the five-pillar scores and weights; run a sensitivity analysis and methodology audit |
| **Event-driven** | Re-score after a major regulatory change, clinical result, cybersecurity incident, safety finding, recession/credit shock, commodity/rate shock, or material M&A |

## 7. Sector-specific diligence cues

This checklist identifies what must be tested before elevating a sector/company conclusion.

| Sector family | Must-test economics | Customer/quality evidence to require | Key reasons a headline can mislead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Software, AI, cloud | Gross margin after infrastructure costs; RPO/retention; cloud capex/depreciation; usage economics | Uptime, outages, support resolution, security events, implementation time, independent review methodology | AI adoption, revenue, and cloud spend do not prove durable cash margins |
| Finance/fintech | Take rate, NIM/cost of funds, credit loss, capital, fraud, unit economics | Complaint rate/account, dispute resolution, authorization rate, trust, digital problem resolution | Payments volume or accounts are not revenue/profit; rates and credit cycles reverse quickly |
| Healthcare/life sciences | Patent/exclusivity-adjusted cash flows, R&D intensity, probability-adjusted pipeline, reimbursement/access | Clinical outcomes, CAHPS/HCAHPS, adherence, shortages, recalls, claim denials/appeals | Approval or satisfaction does not establish commercial success or shareholder return |
| Utilities/climate | Contract/PPA tenor, rate-base growth, cost of capital, merchant exposure, availability/capacity factor | SAIDI/SAIFI/CAIDI, billing/value, restoration time, complaints, safety | Capacity/pipeline does not equal operating cash flow; policy/rate/permit changes can dominate |
| Logistics/transport/auto | Utilization, yield, unit cost, fuel/labor, capacity, capex, inventory/warranty | On-time service, claims/damage, charging uptime, range/service experience, recalls | Revenue may rise while operating profit falls in a freight/price/capacity cycle |
| Defense/aerospace | Funded backlog, contract type, estimate-at-completion, aftermarket mix, delivery/certification | Government audit outcomes, safety/quality, schedule, availability | Backlog includes uncertain options and cannot override losses on problem programs |
| Consumer/commerce/media/travel | Contribution margin after acquisition/fulfillment/content, repeat rate, ad yield, loyalty, returns | ACSI/study score, delivery/return quality, reviews with scope, churn, complaints | GMV/bookings/users/viewing may be subsidized or low-margin; content/hit risk matters |
| Property/construction/industrials/materials | ROIC across cycle, backlog conversion, leverage, utilization, project returns, commodity pass-through | Defects/rework, warranty, on-time delivery, uptime, response, customer concentration | Construction spend/orders and announced capacity are not cash generation |
| Services/education | Utilization, pricing, labor leverage, recurring mix, enrollment/outcomes | Completion/persistence, refund, verified outcomes, NPS/SLAs | Public education spend/employment growth does not automatically accrue to vendors |

## 8. Limitations and next steps

### Material limitations

- **No single statistical industry exists for several labels.** “Technology,” “healthcare,” “financial services,” “energy,” and “consumer” contain different regulatory, accounting, and capital-intensity models.
- **Candidate lists are not measured rankings.** They are research universes generated from scale, relevance, and public disclosure, and include companies with different geographies and fiscal years.
- **The underlying evidence includes forecasts, estimates, audited results, issuer KPIs, and survey scores.** These cannot be blended without labels and source-specific caveats.
- **Customer-quality data are sparse in B2B markets.** Vendor-reported satisfaction or self-selected reviews should receive lower evidence weight than transparent, independent studies.
- **Global comparison is limited.** U.S. government data are strong for many sectors but may not describe global demand; foreign issuers require currency, fiscal-period, and local-regulatory normalization.
- **No valuation work is included.** A high-quality sector may have unattractive current valuations, and a lower-ranked sector may contain exceptional businesses.
- **The score is not predictive.** It is a transparent prioritization framework subject to macro shocks, policy changes, disruption, safety events, litigation, and management execution.

### Recommended next 90-day program

1. **Create the master data dictionary** in Section 5.2 and load five years of annual/LTM data for the top eight provisional sectors.
2. **Split each into homogeneous peer groups** and create a company scorecard only after metric definitions are aligned.
3. **Collect independent customer evidence** for every peer group, including a methodology/denominator field; flag sectors where such evidence is unavailable.
4. **Run a profitability-through-the-cycle review:** normalize commodity, credit, freight, rate, and capex cycles; reconcile GAAP/non-GAAP measures.
5. **Publish score, confidence, and sensitivity results together.** Do not promote any company/sector conclusion when confidence is low or rank is weight-sensitive.
6. **Add a separate valuation and risk workstream** before an investment conclusion, with explicit assumptions and scenario ranges.

## Appendix A — evidence labeling standard

| Label | Meaning | Permitted use in scorecard |
|---|---|---|
| **Official actual** | Government/regulator data with released methodology | High-authority market/regulatory input; note revisions |
| **Audited issuer actual** | Filing/annual report subject to audit/filing controls | Primary financial input, after definition normalization |
| **Issuer KPI** | Company-defined operating metric | Useful but do not treat as independently comparable without definitions |
| **Independent survey** | Third-party study with stated sample/methodology | Customer-quality input only within study scope |
| **Forecast / scenario** | External or issuer projection | Opportunity context; never substitute for realized financial performance |
| **Industry-body estimate** | Association or commissioned economic model | Contextual input; identify methodology and assumptions |
| **Review / anecdote** | App-store/review/social/media observation | Qualitative early-warning signal; not a standalone score basis |
