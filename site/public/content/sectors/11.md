# Telecommunications — 20/20 data refinery sector report

## Sector conclusion

**Telecommunications is financially attractive in the 2020s because it combines recurring connectivity revenue with essential, increasingly data-intensive usage, high infrastructure barriers, and several monetization layers (mobile, fixed broadband, enterprise networking, IoT, wholesale and network access).** The strongest evidence is not that every telecom company is highly profitable: legacy wireline and heavily indebted operators can underperform. Rather, measured evidence points to a durable cash-flow opportunity in scaled wireless, fiber/fixed broadband, towers/wholesale infrastructure and selected enterprise-networking niches.

The sector is best treated as a **quality-and-subsector selection exercise**, not a blanket ranking. OECD data show communication-sector investment in OECD economies reached **USD PPP 290 billion in 2023**, up **18% over 2018–23**; communication-service-provider revenue grew only **1.3% CAGR** over that period, while investment/revenue rose from **16.5% to 19%**, reflecting the capital intensity of fiber, 5G and legacy-network replacement. 

## Why it can be lucrative/profitable in the 2020s

1. **Essential recurring demand and rising data intensity.** Connectivity supports remote work, streaming, cloud applications, digital commerce and connected devices. OECD reports that gigabit fixed-broadband take-up grew strongly from 2019 to 2023 and says demand is expected to rise as business processes and data-intensive applications move online. The same source identifies fiber, 5G, cloud/edge integration, virtualization and AI-assisted network management as current investment themes. 

2. **Scale economics and high barriers to entry.** Spectrum licenses, nationwide radio access networks, fiber backhaul, poles/sites, billing systems and customer acquisition create large fixed costs. Once a network is built, incremental data usage can often be served at attractive contribution margins, although spectrum, tower leases, energy, maintenance, handset subsidies and upgrade capex remain material costs. This is a judgment grounded in operators’ disclosed capital intensity and margins, not a universal industry statistic.

3. **Pricing, mix and convergence can lift revenue per customer.** Operators can monetize more data, premium tiers, device financing, roaming, content/perks, fixed-wireless access (FWA), fiber bundles, business services and wholesale access. Verizon reported 2024 wireless-service revenue of **$65.374 billion**, up **3.2%**, and FWA broadband connections of **2.714 million**, up **45.4%** year over year; it also reported total broadband connections up **10.6%** to **10.014 million**. [Verizon 2024 Form 10-K](https://www.verizon.com/about/sites/default/files/2024-Annual-Report-on-Form-10k.pdf)

4. **Demonstrated operating leverage in scaled wireless.** AT&T reported 2024 Mobility revenue of **$85.255 billion**, Mobility operating income of **$26.314 billion**, a **30.9% operating margin** and **42.8% EBITDA margin**. Mobility subscribers reached **117.851 million**, while postpaid phone churn was **0.76%**. These are company-reported figures and are evidence of profitability at scale, not a sector-wide average. [AT&T 2024 Annual Report](https://investors.att.com/~/media/Files/A/ATT-IR-V2/financial-reports/annual-reports/2024/complete-annual-report-2024.pdf)

5. **Strong cash generation at a scaled challenger.** T-Mobile’s 2024 SEC filing reports **$66.178 billion service revenue**, **$31.864 billion adjusted EBITDA**, **$17.032 billion adjusted free cash flow**, and operating income up **26%** to **$18.010 billion**. Adjusted metrics are non-GAAP measures; they should be reconciled to GAAP and compared carefully across companies. 

6. **Technology migration creates investable growth pockets.** Fiber upgrades, 5G mid-band capacity, FWA, private networks, IoT, cloud/edge, network APIs and AI-enabled operations are the most relevant growth mechanisms. OECD specifically notes that network investment has been driven by broadband expansion/upgrades and decommissioning of copper DSL and 2G/3G networks. 

## Most relevant industries / subsectors

- **Mobile network operators (MNOs):** postpaid and prepaid voice/data, 4G/5G, roaming, device plans and enterprise mobility. Economics depend on churn, ARPU, spectrum and network quality.
- **Fixed broadband and fiber:** FTTH, cable broadband, FWA and converged household connectivity. Fiber has high upfront capex but potentially long asset lives, lower maintenance than copper and strong retention when service quality is good.
- **Telecom towers and site infrastructure:** towers, small cells, fiber backhaul and neutral-host infrastructure. These are infrastructure/wholesale businesses with contracted recurring rent, but growth depends on tenant additions and carrier capex.
- **Enterprise communications and managed networks:** SD-WAN, private 5G, cybersecurity, cloud connectivity, unified communications and public-sector networks. Higher complexity and competition can make returns more variable than consumer wireless.
- **Satellite and space connectivity:** direct-to-device, broadband satellite and backhaul. Strategic relevance is high, but economics are more capital-intensive and technology/launch dependent; treat as a higher-risk candidate area.
- **Network equipment, software and services:** radio access, optical transport, routing, OSS/BSS, cloud-native core and network automation. These are telecom-adjacent suppliers rather than carriers; customer concentration and carrier capex cycles matter.
- **MVNOs and wholesale connectivity:** brands reselling MNO capacity. They can score well on value and customer experience, but generally lack the same network-asset economics and depend on wholesale agreements.

## Candidate leading companies

These are **candidate names for screening**, not an invented ranking. The evidence below shows scale, profitability, or strategic relevance; a full investment screen would still require valuation, balance-sheet, spectrum, regulatory and execution analysis.

- **Verizon Communications (VZ):** 2024 Consumer revenue **$102.9 billion**, approximately **115 million wireless retail connections** and approximately **10 million broadband connections**; 2024 consolidated operating income was **$28.686 billion** and operating cash flow **$36.912 billion**. [Verizon 2024 Form 10-K](https://www.verizon.com/about/sites/default/files/2024-Annual-Report-on-Form-10k.pdf)
- **AT&T (T):** 2024 Mobility revenue **$85.255 billion**, Mobility operating income **$26.314 billion**, **42.8% Mobility EBITDA margin**, and **117.851 million Mobility subscribers**. [AT&T 2024 Annual Report](https://investors.att.com/~/media/Files/A/ATT-IR-V2/financial-reports/annual-reports/2024/complete-annual-report-2024.pdf)
- **T-Mobile US (TMUS):** 2024 total service revenue **$66.178 billion**, adjusted EBITDA **$31.864 billion**, adjusted free cash flow **$17.032 billion** and net income **$11.339 billion**. It also markets 5G-based High Speed Internet using excess network capacity. 
- **Deutsche Telekom (DTEGY/DTE):** relevant global incumbent and parent exposure to T-Mobile US; its U.S. operating segment reported 2024 adjusted EBITDA after leases margin of **38.0%** in the cited annual-report material. [Deutsche Telekom 2024 annual report, U.S. segment](https://report.telekom.com/annual-report-2024/management-report/development-of-business-in-the-operating-segments/united-states.html)
- **American Tower (AMT) and Crown Castle (CCI):** candidate tower-infrastructure screens because tower landlords monetize recurring carrier tenancy rather than retail subscribers. Validate current tenant growth, churn, leverage and fiber exposure from each company’s free annual report before ranking.
- **Comcast (CMCSA), Charter Communications (CHTR), Vodafone Group (VOD), Orange (ORA), Telefónica (TEF), Nippon Telegraph and Telephone (NTT), China Mobile (0941.HK) and Bharti Airtel (BHARTIARTL):** candidate fixed/mobile operator screens across major markets. Their comparability is limited by accounting, regulation, currency and market structure; use local filings and do not infer a global ranking from size alone.
- **Nokia (NOK) and Ericsson (ERIC):** candidate network-equipment screens. Their economics are tied to carrier capex cycles and product mix rather than recurring retail subscriber revenue; assess order intake, margins, R&D and customer concentration separately.

## Customer-quality and satisfaction signals

**Measured satisfaction.** ACSI’s 2023–24 wireless study, based on **12,414 customers** contacted April 2023–March 2024, found overall wireless-service satisfaction rose **3% to 76/100**. MNOs and full-service MVNOs scored **76**, while value MVNOs scored **80**. Among MNOs, AT&T scored **78**, T-Mobile **77**, and Verizon **74**. AT&T led the study’s call-quality score at **82** and network-capability score at **83**; T-Mobile scored 80 on both, and Verizon 80 and 79 respectively. These are survey scores, not proof of future financial performance. 

**Measured network problems.** J.D. Power’s 2024 U.S. Wireless Network Quality Performance Study—Volume 2 used **25,597 wireless customers**, fielded January–June 2024, and reports problems per 100 connections (PP100) across six U.S. regions. Verizon ranked highest in the Mid-Atlantic at **8 PP100**, Northeast at **7**, Southeast at **8** and West at **9**; AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon tied in the Southwest at **10 PP100**. These are regional results and should not be converted into an overall national ranking. 

**Measured fixed-broadband performance.** The FCC’s Thirteenth Measuring Broadband America report evaluated 10 ISPs and 12 ISP/technology configurations. Weighted average advertised download speed was **467 Mbps**, up **52%** from the prior report and **141%** from the report before that. Eight tested ISP/technology configurations measured at least **100% of advertised speed during peak hours**; DSL configurations achieved **63%–72%** on the FCC’s 80/80 consistency metric. The report also measures latency and packet loss. Note that the underlying measurements were collected in 2022 and the report is a performance benchmark, not a current satisfaction survey. 

**Operator quality/retention KPIs.** For a data refinery, collect postpaid phone churn, total churn, net adds, ARPU, broadband net adds, complaints, outage duration, coverage/speed tests, NPS/CSAT and service-revenue growth. AT&T’s 2024 postpaid phone churn of **0.76%** and Verizon’s postpaid phone churn of **0.84%** are useful company-reported retention signals, but definitions differ and should not be compared without checking each filing’s methodology. [AT&T Annual Report](https://investors.att.com/~/media/Files/A/ATT-IR-V2/financial-reports/annual-reports/2024/complete-annual-report-2024.pdf) [Verizon Annual Report](https://www.verizon.com/about/sites/default/files/2024-Annual-Report-on-Form-10k.pdf)

## Suggested 20/20 data fields

- Operator, country, ticker, fiscal year end and reporting currency.
- Mobile subscribers by postpaid/prepaid/reseller; broadband subscribers by fiber/cable/FWA/DSL.
- Service revenue, equipment revenue, ARPU, churn, net adds, subscriber growth and revenue growth.
- EBITDA/operating margin, operating cash flow, free cash flow, capex, spectrum payments, net debt and interest expense.
- Network quality: coverage, median/advertised speed, latency, outages, PP100, complaints and regulator enforcement.
- Customer signals: ACSI/J.D. Power or local equivalent, NPS/CSAT, support satisfaction, price/value perception and switching intent.
- Asset mix: owned spectrum, fiber passings, tower tenancy, wholesale exposure, enterprise/private-network revenue and legacy-network exposure.
- Risk flags: leverage, capex intensity, spectrum renewal, regulatory remedies, privacy/security incidents, handset subsidies, competitive pricing and technology transition costs.

## Caveats

- Telecom revenue growth can be modest even while EBITDA and free cash flow improve through pricing, mix, cost control or merger synergies; examine both growth and capital intensity.
- Reported EBITDA and adjusted free cash flow are not always GAAP measures. Reconcile definitions before cross-company comparisons.
- Company subscriber and churn definitions differ, and some operators change treatment of connected devices or reseller customers.
- Customer surveys are geographically and methodologically bounded. ACSI and J.D. Power results are useful signals, not universal rankings or guarantees of investment returns.
- OECD and FCC data are excellent sector/infrastructure benchmarks but may lag the report date or use different coverage than a current company filing.
- Towers, satellite, equipment vendors, MVNOs and carriers have different business models; they should not be ranked on one common margin or subscriber metric.
